REVIEWS for docs/reports/ziggurat-mineshaft-2026-10-15.md (final: Codex APPROVE r5+r6, Claude APPROVE r3+r5)

================ CODEX r1 (codex exec) ================
1. **P1 — Start-position reconstruction subtracts the duration twice.**  
   `bots/src/logger.mjs:59` sets `@timestamp` to **startedAt**, not completion time. M:111 subtracts `duration_ms + 500` from that start timestamp. Furthermore, skill rows contain completion snapshots (`bots/src/runner.mjs:431`), while M:122 indexes those snapshots by the start timestamp. The resulting history is neither chronological observation history nor reliable pre-call position history. Reconstruct observation times by row type, report missing/stale positions, and recalculate descent distances, depth bands, session starts, and catchment estimates.

2. **P1 — The shaft adoption rule excludes the dominant measured request pattern.**  
   R:120 says 75% of calls request less than two blocks of descent; R:508 admits shaft routing only for requests at least eight blocks down. Grouping short calls into sessions does not make any individual call eligible. R:527 nevertheless applies a 50–75% saving to the entire near-home session yield. Measure eligible **calls and their yield**, or specify deterministic routing that actually intercepts incremental requests. Also handle targets below the shaft bottom: bottom 16, target −20 gives a residual of 36, contradicting “≤7” at R:514.

3. **P1 — Exit credit can credit construction that has not happened.**  
   T6 proposes debt zero after a “walk-profile” probe (R:498). The existing walk profile permits towers and scaffolding (`bots/src/index.mjs:367–387`). A read-only planner can therefore return a route containing future placements; read-only describes the probe’s effects, not the returned route’s requirements. Require a successful, loaded-world, **zero-break/zero-place** route to an intact ascent and safe collar exit. Revalidate the return route for each proposed descent step, while retaining health and working-tool requirements.

4. **P1 — Junk-well interception creates an inventory deadlock.**  
   R:386–389 withholds stone whenever an open segment is reachable, but monument work can still be disallowed by its 16-block minimum, cooldown, hourly budget, lease contention, or ready conversion (R:379–382). A full bag can then retain unusable surplus while a conversion cannot make room. This is precisely the existing ordering trap documented at `bots/src/cognitive.mjs:842–846`. Suppress disposal only when an executable material-consuming visit is admitted, with deterministic overflow fallback; test full-bag conversion, exhausted budget, and unavailable-lease chains.

5. **P1 — Independent segment advancement invalidates the construction safety argument.**  
   R:360 requires only the cells below a segment to be complete. One quadrant can therefore rise several layers while another remains low, creating unfinished internal walls and drops exceeding two blocks. Quadrants also meet at the centre; they do not keep builders 20 blocks apart (R:361). Add a global layer barrier or prove safe asynchronous schedules, including access stairs, occupied cells, collision avoidance, and interrupted builds. A serial centre-out stand simulation does not prove concurrent construction safe.

6. **P1 — Monument protection names remedies without establishing their capability.**  
   “A route … always exists,” “walk 4 off the apron,” and “the bot pillars” (R:412–424) do not follow from a solid final shape. Foundations can border existing underground pockets; neighbouring terrain can block travel; a trapped bot can lack pillar material. `pillarOut` explicitly refuses insufficient inventory (`bots/src/reflex.mjs:5066–5080`). Test partial construction and adjacent entombment with exhausted inventory, blocked overhead, and unloaded terrain. Admission must preserve an executable exit before protection removes the alternatives.

7. **P1 — Shaft geometry and safety are not yet a complete executable specification.**  
   R:460 requires a solid perimeter at every level, while R:464 requires openings into landing rooms. Those portals need explicit geometry and protection semantics. The 4×3 column survey (R:485) does not cover the rooms, stubs, or newly exposed liquid faces. T9 reserves lining and ladders, but not the tool capability and verified return path needed throughout a partially completed level. Specify portals, terminal landings, excavation envelopes, and safe interruption states, then demonstrate survey → line → dig → ladder → retreat under failures. T8’s “cannot open into liquid later” does not follow from protecting the shaft lining when its landing passages remain open.

8. **P2 — Pickup positions are flush positions, not pickup locations.**  
   M:99–101 assigns every group in a summary the summary row’s `bot.pos`. The logger accumulates pickups and emits later (`bots/src/pickuplog.mjs:335,385,422–438`); `bots/src/index.mjs:989` snapshots the bot at that later emission. Thus the 88% underground share and near-home pickup yields are not spatial measurements of the underlying pickups. Aggregate location at collection time, or label these as flush-location proxies and remove precise catchment claims.

9. **P2 — Pickup ownership does not establish dig cause or necessity.**  
   `attributeTo` records the current holder/skill (`bots/src/pickuplog.mjs:74–78`), not the producer of the item. Someone else’s drops can be collected during **any** skill, not just idle/home. Likewise, an iron-gather label does not prove an ore-tunnel excavation, and a survival label does not prove that the excavation was necessary. “Every surface-start descent is a new staircase” also overreaches: `mine` skips already-air cells (`bots/src/skills.mjs:10104–10117`). Report activity-attributed collections; reserve causal and “needed” judgements for traced digs or behavioural evidence.

10. **P2 — Session construction does not implement the stated definition.**  
    M:138–153 groups only mine rows, so intervening surface, home, gather, death, or restart activity cannot terminate a session. Two separate descents within ten minutes become one session credited to its first position. M:156 also treats valid y=0 as missing through `or 999`. Split on intervening activities and discontinuities, use explicit null checks, and publish the 5/10/20-minute sensitivity results already requested by the script.

11. **P2 — Savings denominators do not match deployment eligibility.**  
    R:307 excludes isolated pools, yet R:527 uses the whole-fleet catchment and R:145–146 divides by 16 towns; the siting work covers 12 shared worlds. Measurement uses distance from **home**, while routing uses distance from the **collar** (R:507), displaced by 17–21 blocks. R:529 additionally multiplies a near-home count by the fleet-wide 88% depth share instead of measuring their intersection. Recalculate savings using shared-world, actual-site, eligible-request denominators.

12. **P2 — Funding projections are not a closed material budget.**  
    M:249–254 sums skill inventory deltas, not town-wide stock change. Withdrawals, deposits, deaths, and pickups outside measured skills require reconciliation before “net” becomes renewable supply. R:626 also says surplus falls roughly proportionally: subtracting the proposed 7.8–11.9k/day cut from the stated 9.1k/day net leaves **+1.3k to −2.8k/day**, before redirects and behavioural changes. The 10–15-day estimate omits the stated foundation allowance. Finally, 3,780 cobble is the skin requirement, not guaranteed total cobble consumption when the core also accepts cobble. Publish per-world balances including chest stock, redirects, foundation, floor priority, and post-change supply.

13. **P2 — Siting does not establish the asserted clearances or labour exposure.**  
    An apron starting at Chebyshev 22 does not clear a radius-16 box’s **+8 read margin**, which reaches 24 (R:314–315). Also, time spent within 48 of home is not time available at a monument whose centre may be 64 away. Distinguish exclusion from read margins, and include travel, loaded-chunk coverage, foundation access, and apron elevation in Z0’s feasibility and labour estimates.

14. **P2 — Record completion and leasing need stronger invariants.**  
    R:351–352 accepts any allowed full cube as “done,” including a granite skin cell that violates the cobble-skin specification. Completion must be role-specific. Exclusive creation of individual lease files does not itself enforce the world-wide three-lease limit or safe reclamation after expiry. Specify atomic admission and stale-owner fencing, and validate crash/restart and simultaneous-acquisition behaviour. The plan also requires a durable home for its `stuck` state and no-progress timing.

15. **P2 — §5.1 needs consistent material sets and executable refusal handling.**  
    The measured “full bag” population counts cobble/deepslate only (M:268–293); the proposed refusal counts “stone-family”; the milestone accepts its `COBBLE` family (`bots/src/milestones.mjs:757–764`). These are different predicates. Sixty-four granite, for example, must not block gathering material needed for that rung. Removing mine credit must also add appropriate withdraw attempt attribution (`milestones.mjs:58`). “Names the blocks held” is not an executable remedy: specify the selected conversion, completion, or disposal action. Re-estimate the withdrawal cut after excluding entombed and unreachable cases.

16. **P2 — §5.2 confuses planner failure with physical impossibility.**  
    `noPath` exhausts the planner’s allowed graph, not every physical walking route. Loaded-world limits, exclusions, movement restrictions, and search bounds matter; partial results also need interpretation. The retry is triggered by inadequate displacement (`bots/src/skills.mjs:499–518`), so executor failure must be distinguished from search failure. Record the correlated plan, profile, goal, bounds, execution outcome, and retry digs. Also, a modest dig surcharge is not automatically a veto: pruning occurs when the relevant cost crosses the threshold. See upstream [A*](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PrismarineJS/mineflayer-pathfinder/2.4.5/lib/astar.js) and [movement costs](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/PrismarineJS/mineflayer-pathfinder/2.4.5/lib/movements.js).

17. **P2 — Canary zeros need explicit observability and exposure controls.**  
    “No reserved cell lost” cannot mean only “later reads as air” (R:439): wrong replacements, broken ladder continuity, unloaded cells, and unread cells need separate outcomes. State verified cells/expected cells, shaft trips, builder-hours, and instrument positive controls. One placement proves liveness, not useful construction throughput. The shaft-use read should also measure eligible requests, actual adoption, completed descents/ascents, and output: reduced mine-attributed cobble alone can reward routing failures that stop mining.

18. **P3 — Reconcile the remaining accounting and reproducibility gaps.**  
    R:82–87 lists outcomes summing to **126,573**, leaving **39 of 126,612** unexplained; the instrument has additional outcome categories. M:83–89 counts pickup rows but does not count successful tail parses, so it does not substantiate “21,817 of 21,817 tails” by itself. The seven-day script ends each day at 23:56 (M:329), which should be explicit. Use the required telemetry reader or document the necessary extension, and retain machine-readable outputs supporting these controls.

**New findings in r2**

1. **P1 — Self-repair can permanently block the global layer barrier.**  
   **R:479–497, 556, 574–579.** An escape may replace monument blocks with dirt or another scaffold material: `pillarOut` accepts such materials (`bots/src/reflex.mjs:3335,5067–5070,5130`). Those replacements are not necessarily valid core blocks, and even valid core material can be invalid skin. Completion rejects them, while the builder never digs and explicitly leaves foreign skin blocks alone. Reopening that layer therefore cannot restore completion; the entire monument stalls.

   Buried air damage also needs a repair-access proof: upper layers remain present, so the original open-slab stand/reachability argument no longer applies. This is a construction deadlock, not proof that target-only protection itself traps the escaping bot. Specify an executable repair policy or an explicit degraded-state policy that cannot indefinitely block unrelated construction. Test escape → replacement → reopen → repair → resume.
