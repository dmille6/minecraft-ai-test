--- Codex r1 (verbatim) ---
1. **P1 — F1’s hold branch is not a safe, complete remedy (§7 F1, lines 585–646).** Clear feet with a dangerous block below does **not** establish solid footing: the bot may be falling over lava, standing on magma, or swimming above magma. The danger arm checks feet **OR below**, without checking support (`bots/src/reflex.mjs:2541–2556`). Sneaking cannot establish missing support, and the proposed hold has no deadline, release condition, or deterministic next action. Meanwhile, `inDanger` suppresses other movement recovery (`reflex.mjs:3035,3342`). Define supported-edge, falling, submerged, and solid-hot-floor cases separately; bound the hold and test control cleanup, skill unwind, and watchdog reservation contention. The proposed chain test also excludes `hold` from its allowed outcomes despite explicitly introducing it.

2. **P1 — F1 verifies a trail destination, not a traversable retreat (§7 F1, lines 573–583).** A safe, supported cell among the last 40 samples may be behind a corner, above a drop, obstructed, or too far away. Turning toward it and holding forward does not retrace the trail. `cellLavaSafe` checks nearby lava; it does not establish body clearance or connectivity (`bots/src/lavaguard.mjs:10–18`). Require a bounded, currently verified sequence of moves, including headroom, support and swept geometry. Define how an in-lava retreat exempts the hazardous origin while validating the remaining route.

3. **P1 — F2’s “exit” can be a sealed chamber, and its promised fixtures do not meet its thresholds (§7 F2, lines 650–705).** More than 24 reachable cells or a cell five blocks away proves room to move, not an exit. A sealed corridor satisfies `far`; an enclosed room satisfies `count`. Conversely, the supplied tunnel fixture extends only three cells, and the stair fixture only two; neither has sky, sufficient distance, or 25 reachable cells (`measure/probe-entombed.mjs:18–26`). F2 therefore cannot deliver its stated results on those fixtures. Its stuck backstop is also conditional: idle, digging and declared-stationary bots reset the timer (`bots/src/reflex.mjs:3339–3342`), and G1 misses healthy trapped bots. Define the intended escape endpoint, deterministically select the route when needed, and test enclosed-room, short-corridor and one-way-drop counterexamples.

4. **P1 — F4’s seal is not yet executable from the specified position (§7 F4, lines 742–765).** “Replaceable lava cell” and “body overlaps no lava” do not establish a reachable placement face, unobstructed interaction, safe footing, sufficient blocks, or a safe approach. Lava behind the next solid head/feet cell may be inaccessible before that cell is opened—the exact ordering the design requires. Specify the placement reference, face, reach, approach and resource checks; an inaccessible seal must turn or stop without opening the barrier. Revalidate before **each** dig, re-dig, placement and movement, rather than only before the step’s first dig. Existing mining performs multiple asynchronous digs and a recovery re-dig (`bots/src/skills.mjs:13021–13025,13148–13158`); poolescape already explicitly requires fresh hazard checks at the swing (`docs/reports/poolescape-01-design-2026-10-22.md:113–115`).

5. **P1 — F4’s return remedy is advisory, and `surface` does not walk the recorded stair (§7 F4, lines 809–815; §7 F6).** Returning a failure naming `surface` does not cause the next decision to execute it. Current `surface` probes altitude goals, can use digging movements, and can invoke `shaftAscend`; it has no certificate-following branch (`bots/src/skills.mjs:15369–15373,15401–15427`). A stop on the first step also has no stair cut by this call. Implement a deterministic return executor and test it from initial refusal, partial descent, depleted resources, zone/keepout restrictions and interrupted execution. Make that executor a prerequisite for granting return credit.

6. **P1 — F6’s debt formula does not price the remaining unverified escape (§7 F6, lines 819–869).** With a certified stair from y=63 to −53, `y_top − y` remains 116: essentially today’s debt, despite the claimed zero-dig return. With a stair starting in an enclosed cave at y=−40, the formula can instead grant substantial credit without proving any route from that cave to safety. The prior shaft certificate certified the landing-to-collar route and collar exit, not merely arrival at an arbitrary top (`docs/reports/ziggurat-mineshaft-2026-10-15.md:783`). Define debt as the unverified remainder beyond a certified safe endpoint, and explicitly specify the separate scaffold and swing policies. “Failed revalidation → today’s debt” is not necessarily safe after earlier credit has already allowed those reserves to be spent.

7. **P1 — F6’s certificate is weaker than the T6 certificate it cites (§7 F6, lines 826–835).** `canDig=false` does not mean zero-place: the walk profile allows towers and scaffold placement (`bots/src/index.mjs:426–450`). F6 also records stair cells but does not require recording/revalidating every node of an alternative probe route or applying the execution-time corridor guard. That guard can reject an otherwise planned route (`index.mjs:1026–1033`; `oretunnel.mjs:83–102`). Use T6’s explicit zero-break/zero-place profile and validate the entire executable return route. Clarify coordinates too: the proposed “tread solid” conflicts with the existing tread/feet cell being opened for occupancy; its supporting floor is a distinct cell (`skills.mjs:14458–14464`). Specify certificate extension across calls—the current mine loop stops after 90 steps (`skills.mjs:12818`).

8. **P1 — The proposed correctness gates cannot be computed from the proposed rows (§7 F1/F4/F6).**
   - F1 records neither trail candidates nor sampled geometry nor actual sprint/jump controls, yet G1–G3 require them (lines 595–616).
   - F4 records lava cells and aggregate counts, but not per-dig observations, placement material/reference, or push geometry needed by G1–G3 (lines 774–789).
   - F6 records a validity conclusion and node count, not the certificate nodes and block observations needed to independently recompute validity (lines 840–856).

   Add bounded structured evidence, operation/certificate IDs and timestamps, plus positive controls demonstrating that each gate detects an injected defect. Re-reading a decision’s own `valid` flag is not independent verification.

9. **P1 — F5 can block escape, and its claimed input and loss instrumentation are incomplete (§7 F5, lines 873–910).** Hard exclusions around remembered lava need an outward-egress rule for bots already inside them, including certificate returns and `keepout_exit`. “Pick another gather target” does not remedy a blocked goto-home or return stair. Specify stale-sighting invalidation after sealing or world changes, and bounded recovery when every target is excluded. Several named input rows do not contain lava coordinates: danger logs only a block name, while stand-off/no-retreat details omit the hazard cell (`bots/src/reflex.mjs:2530,2538,2554`). World-facts contention can skip writes (`bots/src/worldfacts.mjs:185,190–197`); the design does not identify the asserted loss counter or a retry/reconciliation mechanism.

10. **P1 — The diamond job lacks enforceable admission, return and resource invariants (§8, lines 939–979).** “At most 2 … a soft preference” does not enforce M8’s cap. A town-wide stair needs an atomic, recoverable occupancy lease covering descent **and ascent**, including renewals, expiry, disconnects and returning miners. The stated 400-use admission budget does not cover its own example of approximately 351 descent digs plus 200 branch digs; pooled uses also do not reserve an iron-or-better pickaxe for diamonds. Define per-action budgets, food/recovery supplies, and a deterministic return from branches and ore approaches to the stair. The certificate currently covers staircase steps only, yet any lava retreat ends the job and may leave that route.

11. **P1 — “Own stair and branches” does not yet establish the diamond tier’s fall-safety exemption (§3, §8, O4).** F4 checks lava but leaves ordinary void handling “as today.” Today the fall probe permits survivable drops rather than requiring a supported, reversible stair (`bots/src/skills.mjs:12931–12950`). A cut can therefore open into a cave and lose the walk-up invariant. Require verified support and reversible movement for every deep-miner step, branch and ore approach before dismissing falls as an independent workstream. This strengthens M7/M9 without changing either owner decision.

12. **P2 — The lava measurement labels and causal claims exceed what the scripts compute (§§0, 2.3–2.4).**
   - `d4.py:28` correctly labels nonhazard feet as **hazard below**, not lava below. The row cannot distinguish lava, magma or another `DANGER_BLOCKS` member underneath; `bubble_column` is the feet block, not the below block.
   - Its conversion window is 20 seconds after the episode’s **first** row (`d4.py:30`), not after any firing or episode end. Later deaths can be labelled “survived.”
   - `d3.py:34–38` only examines mine/gather skills starting within 60 seconds. Longer-running digs are missed even when their execution overlaps the last 30 seconds. Thus 16 is not an established upper bound on dig-exposed deaths.
   - “Seen” means a warning somewhere in the preceding 150-second context, without matching the warned cell to the fatal hazard.
   - `d5.py:25–31` selects the first observed health below 20 in the final 12 seconds, without requiring a preceding full-health observation; that is not necessarily first damage.

   Rename these measures precisely, fix execution-interval matching and episode censoring, and separate temporal association from the claim that the reflex was the last mover.

13. **P2 — Entombment prevalence is supported; resolved episodes, causal dominance and time cost are not (§4).** `e1.py:26` implements the stated proximity/gap grouping, but it does not split on death, respawn or observed recovery. “Cleared” is merely absence of a matched death/unrecoverable row; unrecoverable matching actually extends 60 seconds past the episode (`e1.py:66–69`). Summed spans can include successful intervening activity, so they are not a demonstrated lower bound on entombed time (`e1.py:88`). Multiplying interrupt count by a median from another window likewise does not measure incremental time lost. The supplied scripts contain no implementation of the 20,000-point baseline or the 17/17 climb association. Supply those analyses, control for bot/world clustering, and qualify “dominant cause” and the 3–6% fleet-time estimate.

14. **P2 — Depth rates are a mismatched exposure proxy, not an estimated deep-miner lifetime (§§0, 3, 8).** `es3.py:20–30` assigns an entire ten-minute bucket using its **event-weighted** average height; `d6.py:17–22` assigns deaths by final height. A brief fatal fall can add a deep death without corresponding deep exposure. The caveat is present, but “dies roughly every 2.6 bot-hours” and its use to quantify M7’s alternative overstate what follows. Report the proxy association or recompute time-weighted, pre-death exposure. Also remove the claim that the measurements do not depend on board-a/placebo-a: the extraction scripts include all pools (`es1.py:5`, `es3.py:17`), and the document itself attributes ten deep deaths to placebo-a.

15. **P2 — F3’s ghost trigger cannot reach its stated ghost action, and its fallback can create another hazard (§7 F3).** A client-air ghost with no nearby falling entity fails the proposed trigger, so “send the dig action anyway” is unreachable (lines 725–729). Digging the feet and stepping down also requires verified support, lava/fall checks and an executable movement; `overheadBreakRisk` alone does not establish those. “Existing arms keep the body” is not a remedy when those arms cannot observe the in-wall condition. Keep F3 investigation-only until the trigger, ownership, server witness and complete fallback chain are demonstrated.

16. **P2 — Single-variable and tier claims need narrower registration (§7).** F5 changes both routing exclusions and explore-target selection; F6 adds torch placement independently of exit credit. Split those effects or explicitly justify an inseparable intervention. F1/F2 are plausible tier-2 candidates only after strong Paper evidence exists; a pure-function boundary or “one conjunct” is insufficient. Spell out the tier-2 conditions—four pools, ≥48 bot-hours, declared exposure, clean baseline, no WATCH/HOLD and the required licence—rather than implying +180 follows from high firing counts. F1 appropriately acknowledges low fleet power, but requiring 20-trial constructed scenes to reproduce observational 10%/49% conversion rates is not a sound positive control; demonstrate the failure mechanism and report trial uncertainty.

17. **P3 — Correct internal counts and reproduction details.** The summary says 77 idle deaths and 302 clear-feet episodes, while §2 reports 81 and 303. `es5.py:17` writes `entomb7d.json`, whereas `e1.py:4` reads `entomb7d.json.gz`; document the missing compression step. The JSON inputs are not included, so this checkout supports auditing the calculations but not independently reproducing the reported totals. Finally, “pathfinder never steps into lava” should describe its planning checks, not guarantee physical execution; the document itself identifies execution failures.

The principal existing-code claims check out: blind sprint-jump, feet **or** below danger trigger, head-level entombment walls, lava counted as hollow, ceiling-only neighbour checks, blind recovery push and the swing formula. Pathfinder planning checks do not guarantee safe physical execution.

1. **P1 — F6’s invalidation remedy cannot get the bot past an unrepairable break (§7 F6; unresolved R1).**  
   Design:963–968 says to walk upward to the break, repair within 30 swings, and—if repair exceeds that budget—apply `surface` from **above** the break. The bot is still **below** it. A missing floor, lava obstruction, protected obstruction or longer blockage need not be repairable within that allowance. The same problem occurs when keepout invalidates a certificate after credit was spent (design:1012).

   Nor does a valid certificate automatically select a return on every ordinary exit-contract refusal: existing `surface` uses altitude probes and potentially `shaftAscend`, not certificate traversal (`bots/src/skills.mjs:15371,15401`). Specify a deterministic return executor for all credited exits and an executable fallback from the actual stranded position. Price scaffold separately as well as swings; today’s contract requires both (`bots/src/exit-contract.mjs:157–173`). A fixed repair allowance is not proof that arbitrary route loss is recoverable.

2. **P1 — F4 ships before the invariant that makes its return executable (§7 F4/F6 and ordering; unresolved R1).**  
   F4 records visited stand cells and promises a zero-break, zero-place return (design:864–871). Existing mining can take survivable drops (`bots/src/skills.mjs:12931–12950`); having stood at both endpoints does not make the reverse move possible. Reversibility arrives only in later F6.

   Even F6 says a non-reversible step “ends the certificate” and subsequent steps start another one (design:942–945). That permits losing the original way home, contrary to the diamond-tier fall exemption. Require reversible support **before entering every step**, make this prerequisite part of F4’s return machinery, and explicitly prohibit deep miners from crossing an uncertified transition. A failed return followed by “the marooned and stranded ladders then have the body” is still an unproven refusal chain.

3. **P1 — F1’s replacement state machine contradicts its own execution and gates (§7 F1; new R2 defects).**  
   - `edge` permits a one-block ascent but forbids jump (design:621,630). Local physics permits only a 0.6-block step without jumping (`bots/node_modules/prismarine-physics/index.js:69`). A full-block exit can therefore be selected but never executed.
