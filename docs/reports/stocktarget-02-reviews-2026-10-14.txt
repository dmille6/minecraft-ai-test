===== CODEX ST2-R1 =====
VERDICT: CHANGE

The rule is the right remedy for the measured small-bag dead end: deterministic counting at town, before milestone work, makes the refusal chain executable. The current implementation has four gaps. Line references below are for `5cd9f6ba`; the variant has the same patch identity.

1. **P2 — Recount bypasses the pending-open interlock.** `bots/src/skills.mjs:3357`  
   `deposit` and `townDeposit` check `bot.cobbleOpenPending`; `countTown` does not. After an earlier open times out but remains unresolved, a later recount can open another container and overwrite the pending promise. A scratch probe with an unresolved pending open observed **one additional open and one count**. This defeats the existing protection against overlapping opens and late-window cleanup interfering with subsequent work.  
   **Fix:** enforce the interlock in the shared reconciliation entry point, and avoid selecting a recount while an open remains pending. Test timeout → next recount → late resolution, with a positive control after settlement.

2. **P2 — A pathfinder interruption poisons the town’s shared container memory.** `bots/src/skills.mjs:3506`, `bots/src/skills.mjs:3522`  
   The reconciliation catch labels every non-abort walk rejection “unreachable.” With the new `remember: true`, `"The goal was changed!"` now writes an `unknown` strike; the scratch probe reproduced this. The deposit recovery explicitly excludes this case using `walkFailure(e) === 'interrupted'`.  
   This composes badly with the full-chest sweep: `containerStatus` becomes `backoff`, the sweep increments `unknown`, and expansion defers. Repeated interruptions at least ten minutes apart can mark an otherwise usable chest unusable for six hours across the town.  
   **Fix:** classify walk failures before recording them. An interruption must end the attempt without a container strike or another walk. Also distinguish a shortened remaining budget from evidence of unreachability. Add a chain test covering interruption → shared memory → deposit sweep, alongside the genuine no-path control.

3. **P2 — Aborted recounts disappear from the read’s population.** `bots/src/skills.mjs:3357`, `scripts/host/stockrecountread.py:92`  
   `_stock_recount` is emitted only after `reconcileCobble` returns. An abort propagates past that emission; the scratch probe recorded **zero recount rows for one invoked, aborted recount**. Thus “one row, always” is false. L1/L2 cannot audit those attempts, and an abort after partial progress can hide actual counting from liveness.  
   **Fix:** emit exactly one terminal row for every started recount, preserving partial `counted`/`tried` totals and an explicit interrupted status while still propagating cancellation. Add interrupted-before-count and interrupted-after-count fixtures, then exercise the read’s gates against those rows.

4. **P3 — Runner decline still charges the look cooldown.** `bots/src/recount.mjs:58`  
   Decline clears `lastIssuedAt` but retains `lastLookAt`. The scratch probe issued an order, returned `runner_busy`, and retried one second later: no order was produced. The existing test checks only the cleared field, so it misses the effective charge.  
   **Fix:** refund or restore both timestamps on runner decline; test the subsequent order decision.

Priority composition otherwise looks reasonable: town deposit wins; recount precedes milestone, compost, well and prune work; successful counts remove their own trigger, while cooldown/backoff bound retries. Shared town memory is appropriate for actual reachability evidence, subject to finding 2. The lazy key lookup is throttled, but `unknownRefusals` is evaluated before the town/cooldown gates and can invoke cached journal readers; the entire check is not limited to once per minute.

Validation used scratch `git archive` exports only, with no network or checkout modifications. Existing stock-target/journal and town-deposit tests passed on both pinned builds; the variant’s digtown tests and the read’s selftest passed. Four added behavioral probes failed as described. Patch identities match, and the digtown instrumentation introduces no additional scheduling interaction identified here. Paper-scene results were not available for verification.

(3) THE REACHABILITY STORE: one P1
P1 bots/src/skills.mjs:3502-3505 (reconcileCobble's fail with remember) and :3522 (the walk catch). With remember=true, EVERY non-abort walk failure becomes 'unreachable' and an 'unknown' strike in the town's shared memory, and so does 'open timed out' / 'unopenable'. The deposit learned not to do this (skills.mjs ~2720-2740, STRIKE_WALK_MIN_MS=30 s, Paper 10-05 "struck a good town chest and closed the bank"). It strikes only after a real travel failure with >= 30 s of walk, and never on:
- an interrupted walk ('goal was changed'/'was stopped': walkFailure 'interrupted', not aborted);
- its own clock;
- a walk that resolved without arriving (treated as no_path only after the arrival check).
The recount walks with at most RECONCILE_WALK_MS = 15 s and has no arrival check before the open, so all three become strikes, from every bot at town, once a minute.
Why it matters: two strikes >= 10 min apart make a GOOD container unusable for 6 h for the whole town:
- the deposit and the town deposit skip it (skills.mjs:6724);
- withdraw's sweep hides it (`shut`, :8966) -- possibly the pickaxe chest;
- the full-chest recovery marks it unusable instead of unknown (:3610), so the town no longer DEFERS and may craft and place a NEW chest. A recount can thus cause chest growth.
That is exactly your question (3): yes, it interferes, through the backoff -> unusable transition.
Fix (pick one):
(a) Strike only what the deposit strikes: walkFailure(e) === 'no_path', or a resolved walk whose eyeToBlock > OPEN_REACH, with the walk bound >= STRIKE_WALK_MIN_MS. Never strike on 'interrupted', a budget timeout or 'open timed out'. Add the arrival check before openContainer.
(b) Keep recount failures OUT of the shared outcome memory: a separate field only stockRecountKeys reads (e.g. entries[k].recountMiss: [t...] with its own 2-strikes/6 h rule), so deposit/withdraw/recovery semantics are untouched.
I'd take (b), with (a)'s arrival check.
Add a test: an interrupted walk and a 15 s clock expiry each leave the town memory unchanged; a real no_path does strike (positive control). Add a mutant that strikes on 'interrupted'.
