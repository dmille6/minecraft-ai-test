# ELK audit: what we store, what we read, and the 35 days we were throwing away

**2026-09-23.** Every number here is a live query against the cluster at 10.0.0.186, not a
reading of documentation. No assumptions.

## Access, and a correction to how it was described

- `mcai_ship` is **write-only** (role `mcai_writer`). It returns **403** on
  `indices:monitor/stats` — it cannot even list indices. Correct least-privilege design.
- The read account is `mcai_ro`, password at `/root/.mcai_ro_password` on the ELK host.
- **There was no Elasticsearch client anywhere in the repo.** `scripts/lib/telemetry.py`
  reads the JSONL FILES, and its own docstring calls Elasticsearch "the authoritative
  archive" for anything wider than a few days. 42.4M documents, nothing querying them.

## 2. We have 50 days of data and have been analysing 15

| source | coverage |
|---|---|
| JSONL rotated generations | **15**: 09-02, then 09-10 → 09-23 — **with a gap** |
| Elasticsearch | **50 unbroken days**, 2026-08-05 → 2026-09-23 |

Total archive: **31,250,449** skill documents, **4,040,039** LLM, **7,128,002** journal =
**42.4M**.

Every "30-day review" this project has produced was computed over whatever the files still
held. The real series was in an index nothing queried. This is the single largest finding
of the audit.

**What it immediately buys**, from the first 50-day aggregation ever run:

| day | gather runs | ok% | dominant failure |
|---|---:|---:|---|
| 08-05 | 141 | 36.2% | **stuck 42%** |
| 08-10 | 3,178 | 13.8% | nothing_found 39% |
| 08-15 | 1,576 | 3.0% | no_safe_target 45% |
| 08-25 | 33,239 | 23.5% | nothing_found 23% |
| 09-01 | 26,786 | 10.5% | no_path 25% |

`stuck` was **42% of gather runs in early August and is now silent** — it appears in
today's silent-instrument list. It is not dead code; the fleet stopped entering that state.
`stagnation` likewise. That is the triage the silent-class question needed, and it is only
answerable with history the files no longer hold.

## 3. The Minecraft server logs have never been shipped

`mcai-mc-server` — **no such index**. The template exists (`apply-mappings.sh` creates
`mcai-mc-*`); the data stream was never created and nothing writes to it.

So the 16 Paper servers' own logs are absent from the archive. Concretely, the
`moved wrongly` warnings measured last night — 226 in one world in 2h20m, concentrated in
specific bots — exist only in `latest.log` on the worlds host, are wiped on restart, and
have no history at all.

## 4. `skill.fail_class` was queryable all along; `skill.detail` never was

- `skill.fail_class: keyword` — **mapped and aggregatable since the mappings were applied.**
  The typed vocabulary was available in ES the whole time, while the Python readers indexed
  only `skill.name` and 42 of 65 scripts fell back to regexing prose.
- `skill.detail: text` with **no `.keyword` subfield** — a terms aggregation on `detail` is
  impossible in ES too. So the field the analysis actually used is the one field that
  cannot be aggregated anywhere.

## 5. The LLM stream is 4M documents nobody has aggregated

`mcai-llm-agents` carries `llm.model`, `llm.latency_ms`, `llm.completion_tokens`,
`llm.prompt_eval_duration_ns`, `llm.eval_duration_ns`, `llm.load_duration_ns`,
`llm.endpoint`, `llm.admission`, `llm.error`, plus the full bot/exp context — 173,635
documents a day. Decision latency is 33–42% of fleet life by the project's own measure and
this is the instrument for it. No script reads it.

## 6. Two scripts default to a forbidden network

- `scripts/selfcheck.py:41` → `http://192.168.192.194:9200`
- `infra/observer/mcai-observer.py:29` → `192.168.193.30`

The owner's standing constraint is **do not use the 192.168.19x network**. These are stale
pointers at the retired instance #2. They should be repointed at 10.0.0.186 or deleted.

## What was built during the audit

- `scripts/lib/es.py` — read-only client. Raises on an ES `error` object rather than
  returning it as data, and never embeds a secret.
- `scripts/esaudit.py` — freshness, volume and mapped fields per stream.
- `scripts/eshistory.py` — real archive span and documents per day.
- `scripts/esfailtrend.py` — the 50-day failure-class series.
