## Exporting frames (for the owner's fast machine)

**Where the raw frames are** (on the showcase VM, 10.0.0.40). They are never graded or captioned:

    /srv/showcase/timelapse/frames/<camera>/<YYYYMMDD>/<YYYYMMDD>T<HHMM>Z.png

- One folder per camera, one subfolder per UTC day. Each file is named by its capture time (UTC, 15-min slots).
- New frames may have a `<stamp>.json` beside them. It holds the caption label, for example
  `{"label": "cropfarm", "project": "cropfarm"}`.
- Event frames carry `-event-<label>` after the stamp. Video jobs skip them.

**Cameras and sizes:**
- Normal cameras: topdown, angled, town, iso, workshop, house1, ziggurat, paths, wall, treefarm, expeditions,
  storehouse, copperhall, patina, grounds, stair. They are 1440x1440 or 1920x1080, about 2.5-4.5 MB per frame.
  Together they came to **3.2 GB for 11-06**.
- 4K cameras: `hero4k` (the town) and `project4k` (the active project). They are 3840x2160, about 10-16 MB per frame.
  Each shoots every 30 min (they alternate), so about 0.6 GB per camera per day, roughly 1.2 GB/day for both.
- The last 14 days keep every frame. Older days keep the first frame of each hour. The NAS keeps everything.

**Copy one day out.** Run this on your machine. It only reads from the VM, over the existing `mike` ssh login:

    rsync -av mike@10.0.0.40:/srv/showcase/timelapse/frames/hero4k/20261107/ ./hero4k-20261107/

To get every camera for one day:

    rsync -avm --include='*/' --include='*/20261107/***' --exclude='*' mike@10.0.0.40:/srv/showcase/timelapse/frames/ ./frames/

**The smoothing we used.** Run it on the copied folder. Frames are named by time, so use a glob. The pipeline picks
the input rate to make about 20 s per day, between 2 and 8 frames/s; 4 is typical:

    ffmpeg -framerate 4 -pattern_type glob -i 'hero4k-20261107/*.png' \
      -vf "eq=contrast=1.06:saturation=1.15:gamma=0.98,colorbalance=rh=0.035:gh=0.01:bh=-0.035:rs=-0.01:bs=0.02,vignette=angle=PI/7,minterpolate=fps=30:mi_mode=mci:mc_mode=aobmc:me_mode=bidir:vsbmc=1:scd=none,format=yuv420p" \
      -c:v libx264 -preset slow -crf 17 -pix_fmt yuv420p -movflags +faststart hero4k-20261107-smooth.mp4

- The part before `minterpolate` is our colour grade. Drop it for the raw look.
- Our videos also drop frames where almost nothing changed, add captions, and hold the first frame for 0.5 s and
  the last for 2.5 s. None of that is in this one-liner.
- Cost on .30 with 4 threads: about 0.6-0.7 s per megapixel per output frame. A 20-s 4K clip is roughly an hour
  there; a fast machine will do it much faster.
