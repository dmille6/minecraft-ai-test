1. **P1 — Row M removes a working composter-build route.** A carried `composter` falls into “everything else → chest,” although `composterBuildPlan` explicitly consumes it as the zero-craft, zero-extra-slot route. Evidence: design:139–147; `bots/src/composter.mjs:245–249`; `bots/src/cognitive.mjs:911,934–951`; `bots/src/stocktarget.mjs:100`.

2. **P1 — Junk precedence must include the active peaceful compost policy.** Row J names `NEVER_KEEP`, but the live compost planner also accepts `PEACEFUL_COMPOST`: for example, `blue_orchid` would otherwise hit M, while surplus `bread` could hit F. Both have an existing disposal consumer. Specify that the switch-aware junk classification overrides F/G/M, and use it in D1 too. `isCompostJunk` alone is insufficient. Evidence: design:144–146,210–211; `bots/src/composter.mjs:82–105`; `bots/src/peacefulkit.mjs:38–54`. This is a concrete route for junk entering chests.

3. **P1 — The sapling keep does not compose with composting.** Example: retain 32 spruce saplings and bank 16 oak because spruce is most-held; at subsequent slot pressure, peaceful composting permits consuming all 32 spruce. The positive control is oak/birch, whose existing reserve is 16. Preserve a species that downstream composting also preserves, and reconcile the documented owner rule retaining oak **and** birch. Banking oak is not inherently prohibited by the junk-well rule—saplings are explicitly protected—but deleting `oak_sapling` from global `NEVER_BANKABLE` can also change wanted-item deposits. Make the exception local to the town allowance. Evidence: design:143; `bots/src/peacefulkit.mjs:110–117`; `bots/src/composter.mjs:104–107`; `bots/src/bankable.mjs:304,353`; `bots/src/well.mjs:126`.

4. **P1 — The guarantee and D3 confuse planning with execution.** A full bag can legitimately return `no_effect` while chest surplus exists: an outstanding counting open, sneaking, or vanished containers already produce that result. D3 would revert these valid waits. Conversely, declaring `chest=0` “by construction” cannot detect the planner omitting a name. Separate planner omissions from execution refusals; independently reconstruct eligible stacks and include missing-row/missing-order detection. D1 also needs complete transfer data: `_town_deposit` detail is truncated to 300 characters. Evidence: design:149–153,194–196,210–215; `bots/src/skills.mjs:7194–7201`; `bots/src/towndeposit.mjs:368`.

5. **P2 — The cleanup estimate ignores threshold and visit composition.** If the first container takes a 34-slot bag to 33, the proposed threshold disables allowance steps on the second-container re-plan. The stay latch then prevents another immediate visit. Moreover, compost and well disposal require ≥34 slots: chest cleanup can disable those subsequent exits, so the projected median of ~18 slots is not established. Specify whether eligibility is latched for the visit; test partial first-container capacity, credit exhaustion, and the later disposal orders. Evidence: design:155–158,191–193; `bots/src/skills.mjs:7080,7264`; `bots/src/towndeposit.mjs:320–322`; `bots/src/composter.mjs:603`; `bots/src/well.mjs:1039`.

6. **P2 — Root cause is substantially right; the evidence supports narrower conclusions.** The empty-plan count branch and falsy `counted` wording are exactly as described; `not_wanted` really excludes otherwise unrequested items. However, W3 states **63/85** counts were zero, so it does not establish failed counting as the cause of all **85/85** no-effects. Also, section 2 says **16**, not 15, no-attempt bots; ten later attempted, leaving six. Reported row totals and successes provide useful positive controls. Distance percentiles and median depth alone do not prove one unchanged position for 24 hours: supply XYZ displacement/unique-position evidence and per-bot timestamp coverage. Keeping verified away/underground bots outside this no-trip change is appropriate. Evidence: design:49–55,82–103; `bots/src/towndeposit.mjs:329–334`; `bots/src/skills.mjs:7196`; `bots/src/bankable.mjs:353`.

7. **P2 — Withdrawal can refill the bag, and full storage remains a terminal wait.** The explicit withdraw verb accepts a requested item without applying its bag allowance; the ten-minute hold protects that newly withdrawn surplus from redeposit. Add a deposit → withdraw → hold-expiry chain and state whether this deliberate request overrides the budget. `prune_town` only selects configured stock groups or well junk, so it cannot clear ordinary allowance backlog. An allowance-only full-chest case therefore has no automatic capacity remedy under the proposed no-new-chest rule; describe it as such. Evidence: design:175; `bots/src/skills.mjs:10247–10255`; `bots/src/withdrawpick.mjs:388`; `bots/src/prune.mjs:76–84`.

8. **P3 — Several safety boundaries are sound, but row P misstates them.** One-use pickaxes contribute zero to `depositSurvivors`; two-use copies contribute positively, so removing only genuine one-use copies does not spend the escape reserve. Keeping three craftable cobble preserves last-swing’s material purpose. However, “best + two spares” is not today’s deposit rule: it retains one usable copy per name, plus worn copies that cannot be banked. Correct that description and test the replacement-craft chain, including away-from-town wear-out. The 26-ladder boundary matches the actual reflex consumer; retaining stations, chest, beds, slabs and trapdoors protects their named consumers. Keep the allowance outside `depositPlan`, before valve fallback, with existing holds, visit credits and stock claims intact. Evidence: `bots/src/toolfor.mjs:60–93,145–154`; `bots/src/toolkit.mjs:53–61`; `bots/src/towndeposit.mjs:193–195,244`; `bots/src/reflex.mjs:3723–3727`; `bots/src/skills.mjs:7106–7118`.

9. **P2 — Tier 2 needs an actual eligibility justification.** Missing `bag_fix` registration fields are not evidence that this change qualifies for another tier. CLAUDE.md positively requires a targeted canary population, DiD, one pool, and the two-death tripwire; it does not establish the cited tier exception. Supply the applicable operator policy and registration. Three successful bank rows exercise neither spent-tool destruction nor the failure chains above. Require exposure for both changed mechanisms, independent defect checks, and explicit KEEP/REVERT/INCONCLUSIVE criteria. Evidence: design:217–228; `CLAUDE.md:169–192,206–211`.
