Apply the same selection policy to **`bots/src/reflex.mjs:1378–1384`**, which prices admission and preemption. === CODEX round 2 (codex exec, read-only) ===
VERDICT: CHANGE

The patches for `fb591ef` and `e630474` are byte-identical. The candidate-selection fix is sound, but the stale-copy fallback leaves two unresolved cases.

- **P1 — Bare-hand pricing does not produce a bare-hand dig.** `bots/src/airpocket.mjs:291–293,330`. When `staleRisk` is true and the roomy budget admits the attempt, the stale item remains held. Mineflayer independently calculates its finish timer from `bot.heldItem`; `heldMs` only changes this wrapper’s budget/deadline. It can therefore still send “finished” at pickaxe speed while the server sees an empty hand—the original ghost-air failure. Confirmed in the installed dependency and [upstream digging implementation](https://github.com/PrismarineJS/mineflayer/blob/master/lib/plugins/digging.js). Establish a trustworthy held tool or an actual empty hand before proceeding, then price that executable state. Also cover failed equips leaving a stale **different-name** tool: the name equality condition currently excludes them.

  **Test gap:** `bots/test/airpocket.test.mjs:884–886` asserts successful rescue, but its fake dig opens the block after a fixed delay regardless of held item or server inventory. L7 proves the price expression matters, not that the fallback works.

- **P2 — Pre-empt-then-refuse remains possible in the newly handled stale-copy case.** `bots/src/reflex.mjs:1378–1384` prices the healthy inventory candidate; `bots/src/airpocket.mjs:291–297` can subsequently price the hand and refuse. With the L3 no-op equip and tight budget, admission can stop the escape, then the step refuses. `reflex.mjs:1424` applies the **60-second failure cooldown**, rather than the five-second admission-refusal cooldown. Sharing candidates eliminates the original filter disagreement, but does not establish the claimed admission/execution guarantee.

  Add a behavioral admission → pre-emption → failed equip → recovery test. Require bounded recovery to a trustworthy tool, or demonstrate a reachable resumed escape after failure. L4 checks wiring only; it does not establish that recovery chain. This is a conditional failure path, not proof that every such bot becomes permanently trapped.

The remaining requested checks:

- **Fast one-use copy beside a slower healthy copy:** selecting the healthy copy, or declining admission when its price exceeds budget, is defensible given the supplied stale-inventory evidence. The client cannot safely assume that last use exists. Both callers now make that same decision, so this case alone creates no candidate-selection dead end. Add a mixed-tier/speed test; the new step tests use identical pickaxes.
- **Imports:** `toolfor.mjs` has no imports, so this new dependency introduces no cycle.
- **Hygiene off:** for valid inventory items, the filter preserves the deployed candidate set, object references, and order; `staleRisk` is disabled. The patch preserves the previous execution behavior.
- **Tests:** L1/L2 cover the central selection regression. L4 strips comments and checks its negative control; L5–L7 use the anchored mutation helper. Their limitations are the execution and recovery cases above.
