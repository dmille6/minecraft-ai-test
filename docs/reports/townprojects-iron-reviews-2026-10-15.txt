1. **P1 — Walking is not yet a guaranteed remedy.** [12.7, lines 1116–1125](docs/reports/townprojects-design-2026-10-14.md#L1116) allows an extension through existing tunnels provided they are straight and two high; it does not require a second walking cell. The ore-tunnel code explicitly supports one-wide tunnels (`bots/src/oretunnel.mjs:188`). Placing the far stop block across that passage can cut off the work site beyond it, and a parked cart introduces another obstruction. Structureguard then prevents removing the reserved obstruction. The original stub’s second lane does not guarantee a bypass throughout the extension. Require a surveyed, protected walking bypass and safe dismount positions, demonstrated with both stop blocks and a parked cart present—including after REVERT. Otherwise reject the site before reservation or placement. The composition table’s “walk back … is the same” is currently unproven.

2. **P2 — R0 can qualify traffic that the built lane cannot serve.** [Lines 1101–1120](docs/reports/townprojects-design-2026-10-14.md#L1101) qualify an entire landing using horizontal haul distance, but R1 stops at the first bend, elevation change or obstruction. A landing with frequent 40-block journeys could consequently receive only the seven-cell stub. Likewise, measured journeys need not have endpoints eligible for `ride_lane`. Require the surveyed, contiguous lane and its eligible journeys to pass the distance/time gate before allocating iron; recheck if construction truncates it. Compare complete mount–ride–dismount journeys against walking, rather than riding speed alone.

3. **P2 — R0’s position rows do not directly measure haul time.** [Lines 1102–1106](docs/reports/townprojects-design-2026-10-14.md#L1102) need an explicit reconstruction and coverage rule. `logger.mjs:59` timestamps skill rows at skill start, while `runner.mjs:433–438` supplies the ending position.
