1. **P1 — H3’s proposed fix cannot repair the claimed failure.**  
   **Evidence:** D:245–249, D:318; `bots/src/skills.mjs:480`, `:650`, `:761–772`, `:2602`, `:14672–14684`; `bots/src/runner.mjs:256–263`.  
   The dig-retry rethrow at line 650 is **inside goto’s outer try**. Its catch at 771 already converts an aborted signal plus raw `GoalChanged` into an error carrying `aborted: true`. It therefore reaches the runner as `interrupted`, not `skill_error`.  
   There is another concrete path: **home → surface → surfaceClimb**, whose catch at 14683 rethrows the raw error when the signal is aborted. That can reach the runner as `skill_error`. Replace the diagnosis and planned change with this path, and reproduce it in the composed test. The historical rows support investigating it, but do not prove every row came from it. Also, M:571 and M:576 show **225 GoalChanged messages and one Timeout**, not 226 GoalChanged errors.

2. **P1 — Lease renewal, expiry, and stranded recovery contradict one another.**  
   **Evidence:** D:350–351, D:386–403, D:420–421; `CLAUDE.md:150–167`.  
   The lease renews per leg, yet TTL expiry is the promised terminal escape from stranded mode. Repeated return attempts could keep renewing it indefinitely. Conversely, expiry removes deterministic return ownership and permits another departure while the first bot remains stranded. “One expedition per town” then limits lease holders, not bots lost outside town. Revocation similarly removes the lease that supposedly gives return legs priority.  
   Specify separate **renewable ownership**, **an absolute trip deadline**, and **persistent return/recovery state**. Define whether unresolved stranding blocks another departure. Test revocation, expiry, restart, repeated failed legs, and two competing bots together. “Wait, then let normal orders take over” does not establish a selected recovery chain.

3. **P2 — H2 is real, but its proposed guard needs an explicit liveness contract.**  
   **Evidence:** D:237–239, D:316–333; `bots/src/watchdog.mjs:79–81`, `:291–320`, `:383–411`; `bots/src/runner.mjs:225–242`.  
   Re-entry is real: the interval does not await `check()`, and the old samples remain while escalation awaits `runner.run()`. However:
   - Stranding sampling currently happens inside `judgeStranding()` through `#strandingVerdict()`, **not** through `#sample()`. Merely leaving the latter running does not preserve stranding history.
   - A hung skill body has an existing runner hard-stop: normally **180 seconds plus 30 seconds grace**, so “judged ≤180 seconds later” is not guaranteed.
   - The pending flag needs unconditional release, including errors, and the document must define arbitration between stranding rescue and stagnation rescue.

   Add controlled-clock tests for a nonsettling skill reaching hard-stop, a rejected escalation, and continued stranding-history accumulation. This is a fixable specification gap, not evidence that an in-flight guard inherently creates an endless hang.

4. **P2 — The “surface-only” return guarantee is stronger than the design supports.**  
   **Evidence:** D:374–387, D:397–405, D:415; `bots/src/skills.mjs:497–499`, `:552–555`, `:676–698`; `docs/reports/ziggurat-mineshaft-2026-10-15.md:783`.  
   `GoalNearXZ` does not constrain elevation, and existing goto can retry with digging or larger drops. Standing on a breadcrumb proves a cell existed; it does not prove the reverse traversal is possible. The proposed six-block-drop scene directly exercises that asymmetry.  
   Define enforceable outbound elevation/escape constraints and reserve accounting, or explicitly describe v1 as bounded-risk exploration rather than guaranteed surface travel. Test an irreversible drop with exhausted scaffolding and tools. The shaft design already distinguishes a verified return route from inventory possession.

5. **P2 — Outpost deposit composition is incomplete; changing the height predicate is insufficient.**  
   **Evidence:** D:392–393, D:452–480; `bots/src/chestfull.mjs:69–78`; `bots/src/skills.mjs:5450–5454`, `:7004–7010`; `bots/src/towndeposit.mjs:39–43`, `:84–86`; `bots/src/admission.mjs:397–412`.  
   `depositTargetOk` really is a home-relative vertical filter. But `farFromBank`, admission’s storage probe, deposit target selection, and `town_deposit` do not all use interchangeable rules. `town_deposit` remains confined to **16 horizontal / 12 vertical blocks of configured home**. The measured “at town” population instead uses 48 horizontal blocks (M:414).  
   Specify the deterministic outpost deposit entry point, anchor ownership, item policy, and matching admission/remedy predicates. Test a different-elevation outpost, full containers, and a blocked return. The shed fallback starts at 34 slots, while expeditions turn back at 30; the intervening refusal chain needs an explicit action. Also correct the blanket claim that `deposit` always refuses far away: admission is conditional, and the skill itself can call home (`skills.mjs:2723`).

6. **P2 — E0 cannot immediately certify its proposed 14-day negative.**  
   **Evidence:** D:290–308; `scripts/lib/estelemetry.py:79–93`.  
   On October 19, a 14-day lookback includes October 7, which the telemetry library explicitly refuses because of duplicate documents. Excluding that day creates unknown coverage, not evidence of no visit. Five-minute position samples also miss intervening visits and do not establish surface reachability.  
   Define coverage validity, backfill/reconciliation or a warm-up period, and distinguish **“not observed visited”** from **“unvisited/reachable.”** A healthy heartbeat alone cannot validate these negatives.

The dependency also replans during execution (`../../../bots/node_modules/mineflayer-pathfinder/index.js:466`).

After a ten-second unwind, a rescue can start ten seconds after the verdict; a defective next tick cancels it five seconds later. That defect is excluded from G3. Conversely, successful ten-to-fourteen-second rescues can count without crossing the next tick.

   Count premature stagnation cancellations regardless of duration. Define successful exposure by an actual watchdog tick while the rescue is active, and include fixtures with delayed handoff and cancellation before ten seconds.

   The arithmetic itself is correct:

   | G1 | 224/803 | 10 | 3.80% |
   | G2, home | 226/1,141 | 14 | 4.55% |
   | G3, historical proxy | 534/1,565 | 8 | 3.55% |

   These calculations validate the formula, not population equivalence. G3’s proxy rate is not measured on its new ≥10-second denominator. G4’s 20 attempts and 10% decline ceiling are engineering thresholds; without a stated p, they do not follow from the advertised formula.

   D:619 reconstructs counts as full stacks. But `inventorySummary()` sums every slot of the same name (`bots/src/state.mjs:8–13`). It loses partial-stack placement and component distinctions. Two partial stacks and one consolidated stack can produce identical telemetry while yielding different free-slot and whole-stack-transfer results.

   Using the real planner on invented packed layouts does not make this a slot-level replay. It can overstate the percentage reaching sixteen free slots—the precise prerequisite K0 decides.

   Require actual slot inventories, or conservative bounds that must pass without assuming consolidation. If consolidation is part of the kit, specify and replay that operation. Include destination capacity, holds and visit limits in the gate’s input contract.

   The underlying code diagnosis is otherwise correct: `bankableInventory` applies the cobble reserve, registered `townKeeps`, per-name usable-tool retention, `not_wanted`, and credit caps before `townDepositPlan` chooses transfers. One per-call policy through both layers is the appropriate design.

3. **P2 — Arrival reconciliation needs a departure guard.**

   D:550–557 permits `bank` from **ANY phase** and checks arrival on every decision. A selected bot already within home’s arrival radius is also in `kit`, and can remain there immediately after entering `out`. Applying the specified predicate sends it straight to `bank → done` without an expedition.

   Restrict this reconciliation to a trip that has departed or has explicitly entered return/recovery. Define pre-departure cancellation separately. Test selection and restart at the exact home point, plus the first outbound decision.

4. **P2 — Subtracting a count does not protect the escape logs from placement.**

   `#escape` subtracts an **aggregate** eight-block reserve (`bots/src/cognitive.mjs:478–485`). Pathfinder independently selects the first available listed material in `getScaffoldingItem()` (`lib/movements.js:152–160`), which execution calls at `index.js:533`.

   An in-memory probe using those actual methods produced:

   ```text
   inventory: 3 oak logs + 16 mossy cobblestone
   reported spendable count after reserving 3: 16
   selected placement material: oak_log
   ```

   That ordering exists in `scaffold.mjs`: logs precede mossy cobblestone. Thus D:702’s count-only override does not establish its promised wood reserve.

   Filter protected materials from both planning inventory and placement selection, or implement a shared per-item reserve. Include mixed-material inventory in X-P6b. The accepted-containment policy is reasonable, but should not conceal avoidable consumption of the promised escape kit.

5. **P2 — The proposed movement hook does not enforce all landing limits or the return reserve.**

   `getMoveDropDown()` checks exclusions on cells beside the **departure**, then emits `blockLand` without applying the exclusion to that landing (`lib/movements.js:475–503`). `getMoveDown()` has the same omission (`:506–520`).

   An in-memory probe of the actual dropdown method, with `maxDropDown=3`, returned:

   ```text
   excluded: y < 9
   inspected cells: y=11,10,9
   emitted landing: y=8
   ```

   Therefore a correctly written `exclusionAreasStep` callback alone does not implement D §6.5’s destination limit. Also, `infiniteLiquidDropdownDistance` defaults to true (`movements.js:108`), so `maxDropDown` alone does not bound water landings.

   Validate emitted destinations for every enabled movement type, and explicitly settle liquid-drop behavior. Separately, enforce projected debt plus scaffold consumption **before** the irreversible movement: D:655–659 checks debt at leg end, allowing one leg to overspend the reserve before turning back.

   The sky-light API claim checks out: mineflayer returns the world’s block, and `prismarine-chunk` populates `block.skyLight`. That establishes availability; the proposed Paper measurement still needs to establish whether the chosen rule is practical.

6. **P2 — `rescue: true` does not bypass cooldown or repeat admission.**

   D:566–568 identifies that flag as the mechanism for exemption “as `home` already is.” In the current code:

   - `home` has `rescue: true` (`skills.mjs:14852`).
   - The flag suppresses persistent failure recording (`lessons.mjs:503`).
   - Admission’s cooldown check is unconditional (`admission.mjs:575–583`).
   - Failed and unknown results call `admission.noteFailure` (`cognitive.mjs:1249`, `:1255`).

   Specify an explicit trip-context admission path covering cooldown and repeat rejection. Marking the skills rescue-class alone leaves the reviewed dead end intact. The planned re-issued-return test should exercise the real admission controller.

7. **P2 — X8 still uses the abandoned primary.**

   D §6.10 correctly measures **gathered** output, but X8 at D:919 closes the work after two inconclusive reads described as “unmoved banked ore + logs.” A trip can improve the new primary while banking stays unchanged because materials remain in bags.

   Align X8 with the gathered-output primary, or explicitly ask the owner to choose a separate banking-based stopping rule.

The runner reservation contract is now adequate for design review: the five declined-set sites, asynchronous acquisition gap, failure bookkeeping and livelock latch paths are accurately identified and explicitly covered. The remaining issues affect gate validity or executable expedition behavior, rather than requiring a different strategic order. K0 and E0 may remain pre-expedition gates; their unfinished implementation is not itself a reason to reject the design.

**P2 — K0 can still pass using fewer occupied slots than the actual bag.**

D:653–655 reconstructs each name with packed stacks plus one extra partial stack and asserts that this “can only understate free slots.” That assertion is false.

For example, **64 retained coal can occupy one stack of 64 or four stacks of 16**. Both produce the same telemetry. Because 64 is a multiple of the stack size, K0 reconstructs one stack and adds no partial stack. It overstates free space by three slots. With nineteen other occupied slots, the reconstructed bag has sixteen free slots and passes; the actual bag has thirteen and fails.

This is supported by:

- `state.mjs:8–13`: `inventorySummary()` merges all slots by item name.
- `towndeposit.mjs:176–179`: transfer eligibility depends on individual whole-stack counts.
- `towndeposit.mjs:202–212`: destination fitting consumes one empty container slot per transferred stack.

**Required design correction:** make K0 use actual slot inventories, a proven worst-case bound over compatible layouts, or an explicitly implemented and replayed consolidation operation. Treat insufficient input as unknown rather than passing. E0/K0 remaining pre-expedition gates is appropriate; prescribing an unsound K0 predicate is the blocker.
