- **P2 — Aborted runs lose their iron credit, allowing repeat draws.** `bots/src/skills.mjs:9600–9607` calculates `creditOut` for telemetry, then rethrows without delivering `kitCredit`. The runner’s exception handler (`bots/src/runner.mjs:259`) preserves only craft tally fields; consequently, `cognitive.mjs:1120` cannot update `this.kitCredit`. Preserve reconciled transfer amounts, remaining credit, and verified kit crafts through exceptions and the runner. Add an abort→runner→real-loop→retry test asserting no second draw. The current abort test checks only the row and absence of drops.

- **P2 — A successful partial withdrawal can consume the ladder’s iron.** `bots/src/skills.mjs:9560` accepts any successful `withdrawVisit`; `:9584` then checks total bag iron, not the toolkit’s funded balance. The reused transfer permits incomplete takes: changed sources are skipped (`:8919`), and verification compares inventory against **actual moved amounts**, not requested amounts. For example, if another bot removes the iron source after planning while sticks remain, a sticks-only transfer can succeed. A bot already carrying four ladder ingots then crafts both tools from those ingots. K1–K4 all pass: tools appeared, and drawing zero iron is not prohibited. Require sufficient verified toolkit credit before each craft, and treat an incomplete iron draw as a retained-credit failure. Test a changed chest source with pre-existing bag iron, alongside a complete-transfer positive control.

- **P3 — The spare statistic includes spent copies.** `scripts/host/toolkitread.py:244` counts every stone pickaxe, although the documented metric is usable stone copies per iron holder. This can present destruction of one-use copies as improvement in usable-spare compliance. Filter stone copies with `rem(c) > FLOOR`; report spent copies separately.

**Deviation A is acceptable as a narrower canary scope.** The changed plan destroys only exactly-one-use stone copies, and only with both a usable iron-or-better pickaxe and a usable stone spare. Those destroyed copies do not count toward the reflex’s above-`HARD_STOP` escape reserve. Existing town deposit selects the best usable copy to retain and applies stock caps. Reusing `wearOutPlan` in craft-room remedies preserves that restriction.

However, this does **not** implement the complete original disposal request: copies with 2–10 uses remain, and cleanup requires inventory pressure. Describe it explicitly as one-use cleanup rather than full spent-pickaxe disposal. Avoiding the incompatible well gate is reasonable for this scoped canary.

The toolkit order is correctly placed after milestone and room orders. The verified-craft contract also correctly prevents a withdrawal alone from satisfying `kit_effect`. Neither protects against the two accounting failures above.
