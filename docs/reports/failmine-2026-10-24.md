# failmine: fleet failures -> clusters -> region cuts -> sandbox scenes (2026-10-24)

OWNER 2026-10-24 (memory `owner-data-uses-1024`, item 2): weekly failure mining. The fleet's deaths and long stuck
states are clustered and ranked. The worst sites are cut from region-file copies into permanent Paper scenes, which
generalises the poolescape-01 method. This is analysis and tooling only: no bot code, no canary slot, and no queue,
scheduler, manifest or env change. Branch `failmine-1024`.

**The scoped exception, and how this respects it.** The 09-30 rule still bars open-ended stuck and drowning hunts. The
tool measures, ranks and cuts. It proposes no fix and starts no investigation. A fix built against a scene needs its own
owner approval, as poolescape-01 and the board-a brief did. Drowning clusters appear in the tables, because they are
measurement. They are cut only when asked (`--families`), and none is in the library.

## 2. The instrument

- **A death's cause** is the server's death message at the head of `skill.detail`. That is world evidence; the cause is
  never taken from what the bot intended.
  - lava: "tried to swim in lava", "...after falling", magma, burns.
  - drowned.
  - fall: "fell from a high place", "impaled on a stalagmite", "doomed to fall".
  - stalactite.
  - suffocated.
- **A death's place** is its `bot.pos`. **Its seed** comes from the world's `level.dat`, read through fmserve `meta`.
- **A still spell** is at least 3 consecutive 10-minute buckets of one bot.
  - Each bucket's positioned rows fit within 3 blocks on every axis.
  - Each bucket's centre lies within 3 blocks of the spell's first.
  - Buckets that straddle the day-by-day read boundary are merged. Before the merge, every week-long spell was capped
    at 24.2 h; a regression test now holds this.
- **Clusters** use single linkage.
  - Deaths: per (seed, family), linked within 6 blocks horizontally and 6 vertically.
  - Spells: per seed, linked within 4 blocks.
  - Death clusters rank by deaths, stuck clusters by bot-hours.
  - The cut comes from the world holding the cluster's newest member, which is the least-drifted copy.
- **Picks.** The weekly run takes the top 3 death clusters with at least 3 deaths, of the families it cuts (drowning only by name, `--families`). It takes the top 2 stuck clusters
  with at least 3 bot-h, and only spells still running at the window's end.
  - An ended spell's world already holds its escape, so its cut would not show the trap.
  - A pick the library already covers (same seed and family, start within 6 blocks) is not cut again.
- **Geometry labels** come from the cut:
  - `wet-under-ceiling`: feet or head in water, and a solid block at most 2 above the feet;
  - `water-pit`: feet in water, head in air, and at least 3 solid walls at head level;
  - `enclosed-dry`: fewer than 6 dry, standable cells reachable without digging;
  - `open`.
- **Intent rows are a reproduction input, never a label.** A death scene replays the skill the dead bot was running
  (`script`), or the path goal its own `_fall_path` row names. The oracle then reads the result from the world.
- **Blind input raises.** Each case below raises, and each guard has a mutant that removes it and turns a named test
  red:
  - `_death` renamed to a near name or a far one;
  - death rows with no position;
  - zero position buckets, or positions covering less than 90% of the active buckets;
  - positions for less than 90% of the bots present;
  - more than 60% still (frozen positions);
  - more than 25% of death messages unclassified;
  - a missing region file, an absent chunk, a chunk that is not `full`, a missing section, or a palette index out of
    range;
  - a scene whose cells hash, start or checks disagree.

## 4. Scenes

**Format** (`failmine-scene/1`, validated by `failmine.py validate`; one implementation of the contract):
- **The cut.** `cells` holds `origin` and `size`, a palette of full block states with properties (water levels,
  waterlogged, facing), and run-length codes, y outer, then z, then x inner.
- **Provenance.** `cells_sha256`, the source region files' sha256, each chunk's save time, the backup day and the tool's
  sha256.
- **The bot.** `bot.pos` is its own positioned row. `bot.bag` is `bot.inventory` with per-tool durability from
  `bot.tools`, plus the held item, health and food.
  - `alternates` holds more starts. A death cluster contributes up to 4 deaths of the same world and, for lava, each
    death's first lava contact: the first `_reflex_danger_block` row naming lava at the feet, with that row's health.
- **`script`.** The replayed skill (sandbox brain), or none, which means the model is off.
- **`oracle`.**
  - `escape`: alive, at least 2 blocks horizontally from the start cell or at least 2 above it, held for 20 s, inside
    the arena; 9 min.
  - `survive`: alive with no death of the family; 6 min.
- **`base`** says what the fleet did. **`checks`** are the key cells the driver re-reads after building. **`fleet_kinds`**
  are the fleet bot's reflex rows there, a fidelity descriptor and never a label. **`proof`** holds the driver runs.

**Driver** (`sandbox/failmine/scene-e2e.mjs <botRoot> <label> <scene> [server] [minutes] [start#]`):
- **Refusals.** It refuses without the `failmine` per-server lock and refuses sandbox4. Python must validate the scene
  first.
- **Building.** It builds the cut cell for cell with `setblock`/`fill ... strict`, so there are no block updates and
  water and lava stand as saved until something touches them.
  - The cut is translated in x and z to 4800..7008 / 6400..7072; y is kept, so depth-dependent code sees the fleet's
    depth.
  - It sits in a 2-block stone shell, open above when the cut's top layer is open.
  - The first fall run put the arena at 8000,8000. The bot measures its border from 0,0, so every relocation was refused
    as "12198 blocks out", a refusal the fleet bot never meets. The arena moved.
- **Fidelity check.** It re-reads the named checks and every 37th cell, and aborts below 98% or on any failed check.
  Every run so far read 100%.
- **The run.** It sets the fleet's gamerules and restores them afterwards, teleports the bot in with the fleet bag, and
  applies the fleet health with `damage`. Then it runs the WHOLE bot, `src/index.mjs`.
- **Outcomes** come from the world: RCON position and health, the death message from the bot's `_death` row (classified
  by failmine.py's own table), and the mechanism overlap with `fleet_kinds`.

## 6. Weekly run and install (staged; the operator runs it)

The two hosts cannot ssh to each other today. So 10.0.0.31 (ES, the cron) gets a dedicated restricted key to 10.0.0.30,
modelled on structcount's Q8 key.
- The key line is `restrict,from="10.0.0.31",command="... fmserve.py"`.
- fmserve answers `meta` (seed, spawn, home and gamerules per world, plus the backup day) and `region <world> <rx> <rz> <backup_done>`
  (the staged copy, by `sudo -n cat`, refused for any other night). Everything else exits 2 with no bytes.
- It answers nothing (exit 4) while a backup run has a `start` without its `good` line.

```
on 10.0.0.31:  bash scripts/host/failmine/install-failmine.sh --on-31          # copy + pinned lib + full tests + key; prints the line
on 10.0.0.30:  bash scripts/host/failmine/install-failmine.sh --on-30 '<line>' # fmserve + positive control (16 worlds) + authorized_keys
on 10.0.0.31:  bash scripts/host/failmine/install-failmine.sh --verify         # allowed answers; 5 forbidden refused; forwarding refused
on 10.0.0.31:  bash scripts/host/failmine/install-failmine.sh --dry-run        # one weekly run into ~/failmine-trial
on 10.0.0.31:  bash scripts/host/failmine/install-failmine.sh --install-cron   # Sundays 06:40Z
```

- **Output.** Each run writes `~/failmine/out/<gen>/{table.txt, clusters.json, meta.json, scenes/*.json}`, and
  `~/failmine/out/LATEST` names the newest. 12 generations are kept.
- **Order of a run.** The window ends at the backup the regions come from. The run asks for meta twice and refuses if
  the staging changed in between, and it deletes the region copies afterwards.
- **Scenes are candidates.** One enters the library (`sandbox/scenes/`, committed) only after a base proof: a human or
  agent runs the driver under the lock, `failmine.py prove`, and `failmine.py index`.
- **How a fix uses the library.** It runs `node sandbox/failmine/library-ab.mjs <base> <candidate> <family>`.
  - PASS needs the base still failing the way the fleet did in the same session, and the candidate passing.
  - A base that passes retires the scene. It never counts as a candidate pass.

## 7. What it is not, and limits

- **No bot code, no world edits outside the sandbox arena, no fleet RCON.** The cuts come only from the staged copy.
- **The cut is a box**, at most 49 x 60 x 49. A route out that leaves the box is cut off by the stone shell, so an
  `escape` means "out of the trap cell", not "home".
- **What is not copied:** block entities (chest contents, furnace state), entities, and light. Biomes are the sandbox's.
- **Stuck scenes run with the model off**, as poolescape's did. The fleet bots had the model on. The reflex rungs are
  the same either way, and the comparison uses reflex rows only.
- **A death is a rare draw.** Fleet bots pass these places many times for each death. A death scene that reproduces in
  none of its starts is reported, and it is not admitted to the library.
