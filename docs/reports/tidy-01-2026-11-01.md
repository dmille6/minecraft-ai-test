# tidy-01: the town-ground tidy (2026-11-01)

Progress entry in the canary queue. Code: `bots/src/tidy.mjs` (survey, grading, record), `bots/src/shop.mjs` (the last project,
no `after`), `bots/src/skills.mjs` `buildTidy` (the visit), `bots/src/paths.mjs` (an optional `lim` for the router; absent =
exactly as before). Offline proof: `bots/scripts/tidy-offline.mjs`. Rollback: `TIDY=off`. Tier 2 (`protected.py`: not protected).

## What it does

A build project like the house, paths and wall (record `project-<home>.tidy`, segments, leases, `_build*` rows `project=tidy`,
3 builders). It is LAST in the order with no `after`, so only a bot that no other project admits works it. On in every fleet
pool, off in the showcase unless `TIDY_POOLS` names it. It reserves nothing: `projectsGuarded` leaves it out, so it adds no
footprint, sapling box, siting zone or input to another project.

1. **Pillars.** A single column that stands 2 or more above **every** one of its 8 neighbours, made of a bot's material (dirt,
   grass on placed dirt, cobblestone, cobbled deepslate, planks, netherrack), is cut top first down to its highest neighbour.
   Stone and other natural ground is left. Pillars taller than 6 are left and named (out of reach of a ground stand).
2. **Pits.** A group of up to 3x3 columns, 2 or more below the 5x5 median round it, whose rim is all read, dry ground, is filled
   up to its lowest rim column. Dirt goes in first, then cobblestone, drawn under the 80% project allowance. A pit is not filled
   when it is beside water, holds a tree, is deeper than 6 (a mine) or is wider than 3. The fills go in **layered** segments:
   layer k waits on layer k-1, so nothing is ever set over an open cell.
3. **Grading the paths' corridors.** This runs when the paths are unsited or have a no-site record. The paths' own router is
   re-run with fill and cut 4, then 6, then 8, with the bots' spoil cuttable, never beside water and filling only into air,
   plants or leaves. The grading never works inside another project's reservation, except within 3 columns of a destination (a project's front approach stands in its own gap). Codex r1 flagged this as a blocker; refusing reservations cost isolated-b its route. Each corridor column is brought to the **lowest** level that any routed brush over it uses. Every candidate
   is checked with the paths' own strict router on the ground the tidy would leave behind. The record is the candidate that
   leaves the fewest required legs unrouted, with fewer cells breaking a tie.
4. It never touches protected zones, plan cells, boxes or plots (the plaza), wells, the composter, the farm or tree farm, any
   project's record cells or reservation (also re-checked at every visit for projects sited since), furniture, trees or water.
5. **Completion.** The record is complete when every cut is down (or this bot gave it up after 3 visits with no stand) and every
   fill is laid (or waived after 5 visits in which it was deferred, the wall's waiver). Completion then sets a paths **no-site**
   record's `retryAt` to now, so the paths re-site on the next order instead of after 24 h. A complete tidy is surveyed again
   after 48 h.

The 2+ steps column is the share of 4-adjacent read ground column pairs inside the square of half-width 32 round home that
differ by 2 or more, out of about 8,000 pairs per town.

**The paths route in 7 of 16 towns after one tidy, against 1 of 16 before.** board-c has nothing to lay, so it counts as
neither. The tidy also cuts the unrouted required legs from 40 to 16 over the 14 towns that had any.

**What did not work, measured first:** pillars and pits alone, the task as given, route **0** more towns. In board-b they
lower the 2+ step share only from 45.9% to 44.2%, and a 5x5 median smoothing that moves 2,600 blocks routes none either. The
fleet towns are **quarried**. In board-b, 29% of column tops are cobblestone and 19% are stone. The legs fail on steps of 3 to 8
between the plaza platform and the dug ground, and the paths' router may not fill or cut more than 2, nor cut through
cobblestone. That is why the grading exists.

Re-surveying the tidied ground for a second and third round (`--rounds 3`) adds almost nothing: 0-161 more cells and no newly
routed town.

**Cost on a bot:** a survey takes 0.05-3.2 s of synchronous work, once per tidy round. The slowest towns run all three grading
allowances.
