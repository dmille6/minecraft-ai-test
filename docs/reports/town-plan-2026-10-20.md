# Town plan: one layout for every town, measured first (2026-10-20, design only, r1)

OWNER 10-20 ~12:30Z: "yes, lets do this". Towns should be organized and easy for the bots to use, and look designed
("build cool structures, survive, and work together"). Approved scope: measure each town as it is, before pictures, a town
TEMPLATE plus a plan per town, a siting file the projects read, sorted deposits, a tidy-up chore, and storehouse-01 moved
from conditional to planned with item frames as section labels. No sorting hoppers. Nothing torn down. It does not
displace the current queue.

COORDINATOR 10-20 (relaying the owner): a new SHOWCASE world starts from this design (branch `showcase-1020`, ~10 bots on
the Studio brain, kept builds only, hourly timelapse from frame 0; its bots start once the plan's first code pieces are
KEPT on the bench). So this plan also covers the GREENFIELD case (§5): the siting file is written at founding, before
any chest, station or well exists, and the first canaries are the pieces a fresh town needs to grow in the right shape.
The showcase site note (`docs/reports/showcase-site-2026-10-20.md` on `showcase-1020`) did not exist when this was
written (checked 12:31Z); §4.6 says how the two documents meet.

This is a plan, not a build. No bot code, no queue entry, no world touched. Everything was read read-only:
- **region files** of all 16 worlds, copied with `cp` from `/srv/block2/<w>/world/{region,entities}` on 10.0.0.30
  (10-20 ~12:12Z) and read from the copies (`town-plan-2026-10-20/tools/townmap.py`);

Tools and the numbers they printed are in `docs/reports/town-plan-2026-10-20/` (`tools/`, `data/`, `before/`).

## 0. Answers first

**1. The towns are not short of storage. They are short of ORDER, and the order problem is about FINDING, not walking.**
- 348 town containers in 16 towns hold 2,956 of 9,396 slots (31%).
- 2,378 of those 2,956 slots (80%) are partial stacks. Merging every item into full stacks would free 1,634 slots (55%).
- 270 of 348 containers (78%) hold items from 3 or more groups (stone, wood, metal, fuel, ...).
- A fully sorted town would need **6-10 containers**; the towns have **11-35**.
- Iron ingots sit in **9-25 containers per town**; the fullest one holds 16-60 of the town's 82-241.
- The walks are short: bots spend 5-17% of their time in town (median 8%), about four-fifths of it within 4 blocks of
  home, and a town chore walks a median 2-8 blocks (the well 17).
- The waste is in the searches: in 24 h, **428 of 660 `toolkit` runs** failed "no town container in reach holds 1 iron
  ingot with 4 to spare (3 tried)", and **441 of 1,582 `smelt_town` runs** found "no coal in the town containers looked in".
  Every one of those towns had iron and coal in the census.

**2. How deposit picks a chest today, plainly.**
- The automatic town deposit (a bag at 34+ slots, at town) uses the **nearest one or two town containers** within 16
  blocks, and puts each surplus stack into an **empty slot**. It never looks at what a chest already holds, and never tops
  up a stack. Every visit therefore starts a new partial stack in whatever chest is closest. That is the scatter.
- The model's `deposit` verb uses the **nearest chest or barrel within 48 blocks of where the bot stands**, and tops up
  partial stacks only inside that one chest. From 30 blocks out that can be a chest it cannot reach: 201 of 406 deposits
  in 24 h failed "No path to the goal".
- Details in §1.2.

**3. The core is full. A separate 9x9 storehouse fits inside the town-storage circle in 0 of 16 towns.**
- The containers sit on or beside the 13x13 home platform (7-31 of each town's 11-35).
- Around them stand 19-75 crafting tables and 6-23 furnaces (769 and 176 at town height in all; 543 and 758 more deep).
- Only 91-318 of the 768 cells inside 16 blocks are free, and the largest free rectangle is 3x3 to 4x8.
- Outside the circle (17-30 blocks) a 9x9 pad fits in only hive-a and hive-b.
- **So the retrofit storehouse is the chests already on the platform, sorted in place and labelled** (§6). A roofed
  storehouse building is the greenfield pattern (§5), and in the bench towns it waits on an owner decision (O3).

**4. The template ("ring@1") is concentric** (§3): the home platform at the centre, storehouse and workshop beside it,
the well at the edge of the no-dig zone, farm and shaft in the ring beyond, the monument/pavilion/hall plots outside 22,
and an expansion gate. Lit 2-wide paths run out from the platform.

**5. The siting file** (§4): one per town, `townplan-<home>.g<N>.json`, new generations written with `link()` like the
blueprint records, lower generation wins an overlap. It is usable before anything exists: plots, cells and groups are
all declared up front.

**6. The first three canaries are the pieces a fresh town needs to grow in the right shape** (§9). All are tier 2:
1. `townplan-01`: the siting file and its planner;
2. `plotplace-01`: new chests, tables, furnaces and the composter go into their plots;
3. `sortdeposit-01`: deposits go to the chest that already holds the item, else to its group's chest.

Then `tidy-01` (tier 3), `workshop-01`, `paths-01`, and `storehouse-01` (tier 3, after pavilion-01 proves the roof).

**7. Item frames cannot be built today** (§1.3, O2). There is no leather in any town container, and 6 leather in 3 bags,
fleet-wide. A frame needs 1 leather. Signs (planks and a stick) are the recommended v1 label, with frames as an upgrade.

### 1.2 How deposit picks a chest today (the code, bots/src at origin/main 48fb1a67)

**`town_deposit`** (skills.mjs `townDeposit` + `townContainers`; the deterministic order when a bag is at 34+ slots in
town):
1. Candidates are the town containers within `STORAGE_REACH` = 16 of the bot (3D) and inside the town (16 horizontal /
   12 vertical of home).
2. They are sorted **nearest first** (ties by x, y, z), and at most `TD_MAX_CONTAINERS` = 2 are taken.
3. A container the town remembers as full, unavailable, unusable or backed off is skipped, as is a chest with a solid lid.
   The second half of a double chest already listed is also skipped.
4. The plan (towndeposit.mjs `townDepositPlan`) banks **whole bag slots**, smallest first, above the keeps (stockpile,
   scaffold, tools, iron ladder, the goal).
5. `bankInto` moves each stack into an **EMPTY container slot** (`emptyDest`): two left clicks, whose result the client can
   predict.
- It never asks what the container already holds. So each deposit is a new partial stack in the nearest chest.

**`deposit`** (skills.mjs `deposit`; the model's verb, also used by recovery and by `smelt_town` to bank copper):
1. With `preferAt` (a recovery's new chest, or a job's chest) that container is used first.
2. Otherwise it takes `findBlock` = the **nearest** chest, barrel or trapped chest **within 48 blocks of where the bot
   stands**. The chest must not be deep (|dy| <= 12 from home), not skipped, and in town if the plan is cobble only.
3. With none in reach it walks home and looks again.
4. mineflayer's `transfer` tops up partial stacks **in that one container** before using empty slots.
5. A full or blocked first chest goes to the full-town recovery. That tries other containers and may place a new chest in
   rings 1-4 around a town chest (chestfull.mjs `SITE_RINGS`). **That is how the platform filled up with chests.**

**The searches that take things out:**
- `withdraw_pick` and the model's `withdraw` use `townSearchOrder` (withdrawpick.mjs), which looks in every container:
  - first those the stock journal says hold the item, by how much they cover;
  - then the unknown ones, nearest first;
  - skipping misses for 60 min.
- `toolkit` (the kit's iron) takes containers **nearest first, at most 3** (`WITHDRAW_CONTAINERS`), and needs one
  container holding the ingots plus `TOWN_IRON_KEEP` = 4 spare.
- `smelt_town`'s `jobWithdraw` opens at most 3 containers. It skips for 2 h any container remembered as holding no copper,
  which is exactly the coal-only chests, and takes coal only after copper is in hand (TP plan §1.2).
- toolkit-03 (queued) moves the kit's iron and the job's coal onto the every-container search. Sorted deposits are the
  complement: with iron in one named chest, the first container looked in is the right one.

## 2. Prior art (searched 10-20; sources with URLs in the reviews file, round 0)

**GDMC, the Settlement Generation Competition** (Salge et al. 2018, arXiv 1803.09853; judges' notes 2021, 2108.02955;
2022 winner van der Staaij et al., 2309.10871).
- Judged on adaptability (fit the terrain without levelling it), functionality (an avatar can move and reach resources),
  narrative and aesthetics.
- Judges preferred:
  - clear zones;
  - a centrepiece at the core;
  - paths that cross rough ground with little terraforming.
- The winning approach grows roads from the flattest spot, adds links that cut travel between random points, and zones
  plots by neighbour type (homes away from industry).
- **Applies:** zones by function, a core landmark, minimal terraforming, plots allocated outward from existing buildings
  at similar height (the HSTEHSTEHSTE 2022 entry's breadth-first plot search).
- **Does not apply:** every GDMC entry places blocks instantly through a world-editing API, with no inventory, travel or
  materials. Here each block is carried and placed by a bot, world edits are forbidden, and nothing is torn down. So the
  template must be cheap and resumable:
  - paths are a shovel's use, not paving;
  - storage starts as the chests that exist.

**Vanilla villages** (minecraft.wiki, Jigsaw structure / Village).
- A centre, then street pieces, then houses, with typed connectors and fallbacks. The village's extent is defined by its
  beds, bell and job blocks, not a radius.
- **Applies:** the plan as a graph of plots with connectors (paths), plots filled as they are needed, and "streets follow
  terrain, buildings stay rigid".

**Copper golem** (Java 1.21.9; minecraft.wiki).
- Takes items from a copper chest and puts them into a chest that is empty or already holds that item.
- This is the game's own "deposit where it already lives" rule, and the precedent for §7.2.
- **Not usable here:** the server is Paper 1.21.8 (PAPER-BUILD.txt) and has no golem. It would also move items without
  the count journal, the same objection as hoppers (TP §7).

**RimWorld stockpiles / Dwarf Fortress** (rimworldwiki Stockpile zone).
- Each zone has a filter and a priority. Idle haulers move stored items to a better zone with room. Small stockpiles go
  next to the workbench.
- **Applies:** the idle tidy-up job (§7.3), and the workshop beside the storehouse.

**Player storage conventions.**
- Categories (building blocks, ores, wood, food, tools, misc), an overflow chest, labels, aisles 2 wide, and the
  most-used chests at the entrance.
- **Applies:** the group list (§4.4), the input/overflow section, and putting metal and fuel nearest the door.

**Bot projects** (mineflayer-collectblock, Voyager, mindcraft, Project Sid arXiv 2411.00114, MineLand, Baritone).
- Deposits go to a listed chest or a chest at a position. **No open-source mineflayer project sorts by category.**
- Project Sid's agents used shared chests and named places, and the authors report the agents lack the spatial
  reasoning to build structures.
- **Applies:** the layout must come from code (a deterministic planner and a file), not from the model. Baritone's
  layer-by-layer schematic build is the model the builder already follows.

**Mechanics** (minecraft.wiki):
- A chest opens unless a redstone conductor is directly above it. Barrels always open.
- A wall sign or an item frame above a chest does not block it.
- Torches cannot stand on `dirt_path`, so lights go beside the path.
- A sorter needs a comparator, which needs nether quartz.
- Peaceful: no hostile mobs, so light is for looks and navigation.

## 3. The template, `ring@1`

All offsets are from home H:
- dx east, dz south;
- Chebyshev distance d = max(|dx|, |dz|);
- s = the platform's top (home.y - 2; on a greenfield site, the ground level the founder picks, §5).

The template has an ORIENTATION: the side (E, S, W or N) the storehouse takes. The planner picks it from the terrain

## 4. The siting file

### 4.1 Name, place, protocol

- **Name and place:** `townplan-<hx>_<hy>_<hz>.g<N>.json`, in the town's state directory (`/var/lib/mcai/_pool-<pool>/`,
  the same directory as `junkwell-plinth-*.json` and `treefarm-*.g*.json`).
- **Generations:** written with `link()` (one writer per N, never pruned), exactly as blueprint.mjs and the tree farm do.
  A file from another world (`world` differs) counts as absent.
- **Readers** take the newest generation that parses. A reader that does not know the `v` treats the plan as absent and
  behaves as today. That is the rollback path.
- **Isolated worlds** keep town state per bot (§1.3: four or five records for one cap). Plans follow the TP rule D2: v1
  writes plans only where `MEMORY_SCOPE=shared`, and isolated bots behave as today.

### 4.3 Who writes it

- **The planner is bot code, deterministic,** and works in any world (the owner's "no world edits" rule: the fix must
  work in any Minecraft world, so a host-side generator cannot be the only source).
- **Every bot computes the same plan** from the same reads. The first bot at town with every plot cell loaded publishes
  gen 1. Unknown cells mean "no plan yet", as the TP siting rule says.
- **Greenfield (`mode: greenfield`).** The plan is written at founding:
  - when a town's first bot sets home and finds no plan, it fits ring@1;
  - it rotates the template (E, S, W, N) and takes the orientation where the storehouse and workshop pads need the least
    fill and no digs;
  - the well, farm and outer plots get sectors on the remaining sides.
- **Retrofit (`mode: retrofit`).** The planner fits around what stands (§6):
  - core = the existing platform;
  - store = the existing container cluster;
  - groups.homes = existing chests, chosen by the rule in §6.1;
  - workshop = the free strip nearest the core that holds 5x3 (or 5x2);
  - well and farm = their records;
  - outer sectors from Z0/M0.
- **This document's per-town plans** (§6) are the same planner's PREVIEW, run offline on the 10-20 copies. The runtime
  planner re-proves every cell.

## 6. Retrofit: the 16 bench towns

### 6.1 The rules (nothing torn down; existing chests stay usable and drain into the plan)

1. **core = the existing platform**, unchanged.
2. **store = the existing container cluster** on and beside the platform. The plan names **group homes among the
   existing chests**, deterministically:
   1. For each group in a fixed order (metal, fuel, stone, wood, tools, farm, valuable, build, earth, misc), the home is
      the town container with the most of that group. Counts come from the stock journal (the counts `count_town`
      keeps); with no count it is unknown.
   2. A container is only one group's home, so ties and conflicts go to the next container, and **double chests count
      once**.
   3. A group with no stock gets the nearest empty or emptiest container in the cluster.
   4. Every other container goes on the `drain` list.
   5. The fixed order puts metal and fuel first, because those two are what the failing searches look for (§1.4).
3. **Labels:** a sign on the block face above or beside each home chest, never on the chest itself (a click on a frame
   or sign must not be the click that opens the chest). There is no wall to hang one on, so the sign goes on a fence
   post or a cobblestone post next to the chest (1 cobble, 1 sign). That post is a structure cell: never directly above
   a container, and refused by the lid rule (a) if it would be.
4. **workshop = the best free strip** nearest the core. The table below gives the largest free rectangle per town;
   12 of 16 towns hold a 5x3 or a 5x2. The 4 that do not (board-b 4x3, board-c 4x4, isolated-a 3x3, placebo-c 3x3) get
   a 4-cell row, or wait for the tidy-up to free cells (stations are not moved without O4).
5. **well, farm, shaft, monument** = their records and Z0/M0 sites. Their sectors come from §1.6.
6. **storehouse-01 as a building** waits on O3 in the bench towns: a 9x9 pad fits only in hive-a and hive-b, and only
   outside the 16 circle.
7. **paths:** spokes from the platform's edge to the well, the farm, the workshop and the gate, where the ground allows.

| placement today | redirect when a plan exists | fallback |
|---|---|---|
| full-town recovery's new chest: rings 1-4 around a town chest (chestfull `pickChestSite`) | **greenfield:** the next free `store` section cell. **Retrofit:** the free cell nearest the cluster that is not a plot or path cell | today's rings, minus plot and path cells (TP rule (a)) |
| a crafting table or furnace placed while the bot is in town (d <= 16): wherever the craft or smelt stands | the next free `work` station cell, if the bot can reach it within the skill's walk budget; **if a workshop station of that kind exists and is reachable, use it and place nothing** | today's placement, but never on a plot or path cell |

- **Outside town, nothing changes.** Mines keep their own tables and furnaces.
- craft keeps taking its table back (`_table_retaken`), except a table placed in a workshop station cell, which stays.

### 7.2 `sortdeposit`: "deposit where it already lives"

**The rule**, for each stack the existing plan banks (the keeps, cobble cap, stock targets and value rules are
unchanged). Destinations, in order:
1. **a town container the stock journal says holds that item**, with room. Top up its partial stack first, then an empty
   slot in it;
2. **the item's group home(s)** (`groups.homes`), top-up then empty slot;
3. **the overflow** (`groups.overflow`);
4. **today's rule**: the nearest town container, empty slot.

Step 1 before step 2 is the copper-golem rule. A container that already holds the item is where a search will look first
(`townSearchOrder` tier 0).

**What it changes in code:**
- `town_deposit`'s `townContainers` becomes "the containers this plan's steps choose". Still nearest-first among equals,
  still skipping full, unavailable or lid-blocked ones.
- `bankInto` gains a TOP-UP move: left-click the bag stack, then left-click the container's partial stack of the same item
  (the server merges up to the max; the remainder stays on the cursor). The rest goes to an empty slot.
  - The cursor rules are unchanged: settle with the server's word before the close, never close loaded.
  - A top-up is predictable: `min(max - have, n)` moves.
- The per-visit budget becomes **at most 3 containers** (was 2) within the same 45 s. Storehouse and cluster containers
  are a few blocks apart, and a 5-group bag needs more than 2.
- **The model's `deposit` verb uses the same destinations when the bot is in town.** Outside town it is unchanged, but
  a chest more than 16 blocks from home is no longer its target when a town plan exists: it walks home first. This
  addresses the 201 "No path" failures, which come from targeting the nearest chest from 31 blocks out.
  - That redirect is its own switch (`SORTDEPOSIT_FAR=0` to disable), and the read reports it separately.

**Before a plan exists** (or `TOWNPLAN=off`), every deposit behaves as today.

### 7.3 `tidy`: the idle tidy-up chore

A deterministic town order, like `town_deposit`, never the model's choice.

**Eligible when all hold:**
- the bot is in town (d <= 8);
- no other town order is eligible (town_deposit, compost, well, withdraw_pick, toolkit, smelt_town run first);
- the bag has at least 4 free slots after the keeps, so bagbudget's allowance is untouched;
- the town is not paused for tidy;
- the per-bot cooldown (20 min) has passed;
- it holds the town's **tidy lease** (one tidier per town, 4 min TTL, renewed per move, like the project lease).

**One visit:**
- at most **4 stacks**, at most **3 containers** opened, 60 s budget;
- the source is the `drain` container (or a home holding a foreign group's stack) with the most foreign stacks within
  8 blocks of the destination;
- the destination is that item's home, by §7.2's order;
- each stack is withdrawn (a claim like withdraw's, recorded in the stock journal) and then deposited.

**Never moved:**
- an item under a live claim (withdraw holds, project allocations, cobblecap claims, toolkit draws);
- a tool at or below toolfor's FLOOR;
- anything from a container outside town.

**Terminal states:**
- a visit that moves nothing (all sources claimed, all homes full) counts a strike;
- 3 strikes in 6 h pause tidy in that town for 6 h (`paused:<reason>`, recorded; TP design 3.2's standalone-job rule).

**Interrupted mid-move:**
- the stacks in the bag are not kept by any keep rule, so the next `town_deposit` banks them by §7.2;
- the tidy row records `stranded:<n>`;
- a test proves the chain (§8 row T3).

**Conservation (gate):** per visit, the bag's start and end are equal for the moved names, and the sum of container
deltas is 0 (server re-open, as `town_deposit` verifies).

### 7.4 `storehouse-01`: planned, not conditional

- **Owner (10-20): move it from conditional (fill > 60%) to planned.** It is now in the build order (§9), after
  pavilion-01. The reason is roof construction: pavilion-01 is the first Paper proof of the terraced roof sequence (ring
  stands, bridged doorway, inner cells from below), and storehouse-01 reuses it.
- **Greenfield shape (`storehouse@1g`):**
  - pad 9x9, step ring, 7x7 walls 2 high, plank roof at s+3 (design 4.2's shell and BOM: 150 cobble, 52 logs);
  - **inside: one row of chests along three walls (13 halves, 351 slots), not 18 two-high barrels**, so the founding chests (§5 F2)
    are already in place and nothing is ever above a container;
  - labels: a sign on the wall above each section;
  - lights: 4 torches.
- **Bench shape:** O3.
- **Labels: signs in v1** (O2).
  - A sign is 6 planks and 1 stick per 3, placed on a wall's side face. Its text is set by the placing bot
    (`bot.updateSign`); the Paper scene must prove the text sticks.
  - Item frames are the upgrade once leather exists (O2). The frame goes on the wall above the section and shows one item
    of the group, which is used up.
- **Count journal:** each container the builder places is observed EMPTY in the journal at its placement. Without that,
  13 new uncounted containers would make cobblecap's completeness set unknown and refuse every cobble deposit until
  `count_town` caught up.

### 7.5 `workshop`: stations in one place, and the town jobs use them

- **Stations:** 2 crafting tables and 3 furnaces in the workshop strip, placed from bags first (178 tables and 87
  furnaces are carried). Materials only if no bot carries one.
- **Use:** `smelt_town`, `make_part` and `make_copper` prefer a workshop furnace, then any furnace within 16 of home.
- **`furnace_drain` drains only workshop furnaces and furnaces within 16 of home** (today: any within 48, which is where
  1,306 of 1,309 runs in 24 h ended without a success). That is a scope change with its own switch (`DRAIN_SCOPE=48`
  restores today's).
- The TP furnace lease is unchanged. Workshop furnaces are leased per session like any furnace, and the deferred
  `yard-01` stays deferred (O1 of the TP plan): the workshop is a station row, not a yard.

### 7.6 `paths`: lit 2-wide spokes

- **Cells:** for each path in the plan, the cells where the top block is `grass_block` or `dirt` with air above become
  `dirt_path`, by a shovel's use (`activateBlock` with a shovel held). That is not a dig: the block stays, and townzone's
  dig guard does not see it. **Paper must confirm** that the zone's hooks do not intercept it.
- Other cells (stone, cobble, holes) are left as they are and only reserved. The town floor fills holes under paths
  first.
- **Lights:** a torch on the ground beside the path every 6 cells, alternating sides, never on the path (torches cannot
  stand on `dirt_path`).
- **Cost:** shovel wear about 1 per cell; torches from the build group (960 banked, 671 carried).
- **Order:** paths are planned at founding; each is built only after the plot it leads to has its first block.
