# leaf-01 at +90: the refusal it removes was load-bearing (read-only note, 19 Sep 14:30 UTC)

Not my canary. Read-only, no fleet action taken. Flagging because the watched line is the project's endpoint metric
and the mechanism is already legible in the read's own output.

`VERDICT NOT_YET (+90) :: deaths 0 (0.000) vs control 0.053/bh | exposure leafread.gather_runs_canary = 209 (min 100)
| WATCH: items gathered/bh`

## The change did what it intended, and the endpoint went the wrong way

leaf-01 lets a `*_leaves` neighbour count as exposing a `*_log`, because `oak_leaves.boundingBox` is `'block'` so a
trunk inside its own canopy read as buried. The buried refusal did fall, exactly as designed:

| line | canary pre -> post | control pre -> post | DiD |
|---|---|---|---|
| buried refusals / bot-h | 3.65 -> 0.87 | 3.88 -> 4.29 | **-3.18** |
| acquired logs / bot-h | 0.64 -> 0.07 | 4.25 -> 4.10 | **-0.42** |

The read's own annotation states the rule this trips: *"a fall here with NO rise in logs is the failure mode, not the
win."* That is the case here. Refusals fell by three per bot-hour and logs did not rise.

## Where the freed attempts went

Canary post conversion classes:

```
failed/no_safe_target 78 | success 45 | failed/no_path 38 | failed/unreachable 25
aborted/interrupted 11 | failed/nothing_found 8 | unknown/collect_budget 4
```

**141 failures against 45 successes.** The refusal was not hiding reachable wood. It was declining targets that the
approach cannot reach anyway, cheaply. Removing it converts a cheap refusal into an expensive failed attempt, and the
guards show the cost landing where you would expect:

- `items gathered/bh` **-63%**, against a guard band of >= -50%. This is the breach.
- `explore/bh` **+81%** -- gather fails, the bot falls back to explore.
- `blocks moved/bh` +25%, `working share` -3%, `immobile` +3.5 pp DiD with 0 newly immobile bots.

This is the same shape as the lava-guard chain: a refusal is an INPUT to the next policy, and what the next policy
does on failure is the thing that must be measured.

## Caveats, stated plainly

- **The log counts are tiny.** One acquired log on the canary post-window, nineteen pre. At that volume logs/bot-h is
  close to unmeasurable and the DiD should not carry the decision on its own. The conversion classes and the -63%
  items line rest on much larger denominators and are the stronger evidence.
- The canary pools were already 6.6x worse at logs than control BEFORE the change (0.64 vs 4.25/bot-h), which is why
  this has to be read as a difference-in-differences and never cross-sectionally.
- Deaths are clean: 0 canary against 0.053/bot-h control.

## The point for the operator

On the registered rules this is one guard breach, so it is a WATCH and the loop will ride to the next read. Worth
knowing before that read: **`items gathered/bh` is the endpoint this project is judged on**, -63% is closer to the
severe line than to the band, and the mechanism that produced it is visible rather than inferred. If it holds at the
next read, the honest reading is REVERT on the mechanism rather than KEEP on a single unbreached guard.

If the hypothesis is worth saving, the shape that would test it is a refusal that declines the target for the reason
the approach actually fails on -- `no_safe_target` is 55% of the canary's failures -- rather than removing the buried
check and letting the approach discover the same thing expensively.
