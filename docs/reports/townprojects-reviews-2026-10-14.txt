=================== CODEX ROUND 1 ===================

1. **P1 — §§4, 4.1–4.3, 9: The buildings cannot be constructed under the stated stand/face rules.** On the pad, feet are at `s+1` and eyes at `s+2.62`. Placing the third vertical log requires clicking a top face at `s+3`; starting the roof requires one at `s+4`. Plinth’s `standFor` and `standOk` explicitly reject top-face clicks from below. The hall’s upward lightning rod has the same problem. **Fix:** add protected elevated stands/scaffolding with a proven return route, or redesign the structures. Require a complete placement sequence on flat terrain and real town fixtures before estimating builder effort.

2. **P1 — §3.3: Netted procurement demand is not a safe disposal floor.** Suppose 64 roof cells remain and the town holds 64 `cut_copper`. Netted demand becomes zero; the proposed zero-base group then permits pruning the entire stack. The parts disappear precisely because production succeeded. **Fix:** separate procurement shortfall from protected project inventory. Allocate existing parts and inputs to the project, and prevent banking/prune/well calculations from treating those allocations as surplus. Test `craft → deposit → prune/well → builder withdraw` through the real chain.

3. **P1 — §§3.3, 5, 9: Banking and stock visibility are prerequisites, not stage-5 additions.** `bankableInventory` rejects unwanted materials before consulting their stock target. `copper_block`, `cut_copper`, `stone_bricks`, and `barrel` are absent from its standing/deposit-always sets; adding targets alone does not make them bankable. The existing count journal records targeted groups, so it also does not supply the proposed complete census of untargeted copper, iron, and fuel. **Fix:** introduce project-aware banking, counting, holds, and delivery in the first stage that needs each material. Demonstrate that stage-1 compaction can actually deposit its output and stage-2 helpers can deliver parts.

4. **P1 — §§3.2–3.3, 5: Raw-BOM admission conflicts with earlier conversions and base caps.** Stage 1 deliberately converts raw copper into blocks, but siting later requires the *raw* BOM to fit stock. A town with a finished roof’s worth of copper blocks could therefore fail admission. The “all intermediate BOMs fit base targets” assertion is also false: hall M needs 88 stone, versus the existing stone target of 64. **Fix:** assess a recipe-aware inventory allocation across finished parts, intermediates, raw inputs, fuel, and bag reserves. Specify bounded intermediate batches or move demand protection earlier; test admission after compaction and after stock pruning.

5. **P1 — §§3.1, 3.4, 8: World-keyed projects leave isolated-town accounting inconsistent.** `cobblecap.mjs` explicitly says isolated bots have separate journals and cannot see each other’s claims. Sharing only the project record and builder lease leaves helper withdrawals, production, pruning, and banking using incompatible views of the same physical containers. **Fix:** share the relevant inventory journal and claims by physical world/town, with migration and restart rules, or exclude isolated worlds until that prerequisite ships. Test concurrent helper/pruner activity across different `MEMORY_POOL` values.

6. **P1 — §8: Pathfinder exclusions do not protect against every direct dig.** The composition table groups `mine` with gather/pathfinder, but `mine` directly calls `bot.dig` for stair cells and retries without consulting movement exclusions. Other direct maintenance digs also need consideration. Townzone’s bounded area cannot protect every project allowed out to radius 48. **Fix:** enforce project protection at each discretionary direct-dig path, including retries and make-room operations, with explicit survival exceptions. Behaviour tests must attempt to mine through a project outside townzone and verify server cells remain intact.

7. **P1 — §§3.4, 5, 8: Stage-1 furnace sharing is not safe merely because output eventually belongs to town.** Existing `smelt` takes pre-existing output, removes incompatible input, and drains furnace slots on exit. Two deterministic smelters—or a project smelter and a milestone smelter—can interfere throughout the operation. This also defeats per-order loss attribution. **Fix:** lease a furnace for the entire existing `smelt` session, including recovery, and make every caller participate. Stage 3 must strictly exclude workshop furnaces from ordinary `smelt`, rather than merely prefer another furnace. Test interruption and competing milestone calls.

8. **P1 — §§3.5–6: Several refusals have no deterministic path back to progress.** “Raw shortage cannot happen” is contradicted by concurrent consumption, reserves, and disposal. Ordinary gathering does not guarantee replenishment of the named shortage. Unknown cells receive no survey trip; blocked cells can stall the fixed WIP queue indefinitely. Project-specific craft-room refusals also cannot rely on `roomOrder`, which currently runs from the milestone’s `ready.noRoom`. **Fix:** specify resumable recovery states for room-making, stock reconciliation, reachable station selection, material acquisition, and surveys; provide explicit abandonment/replanning for permanent obstruction. Exercise these through CognitiveLoop with full bags, full containers, absent stations, and a pending milestone.

9. **P2 — §§2, 4.3–4.4: The copper BOM ignores crafting batch sizes.** The supplied 1.21.8 registry confirms `4 copper_block → 4 cut_copper`. Producing 63 roof blocks requires 64 copper blocks, leaving one cut-copper spare. From raw materials, M therefore needs **579 copper**, and **667 smelts** including its 88 stone. S/L require **327/903 copper**, respectively. Ideal pooled fuel for M is **84 coal for smelting plus one for torches**, before scheduling losses. `cut_copper` also fits the inventory’s 2×2 grid, contrary to the table’s “needs a table” entry. **Fix:** derive integer BOMs, leftovers, fuel, and sizing thresholds from the chosen recipe route.

10. **P2 — §§4.2, 4.4, 5: Blast-furnace arithmetic is inconsistent.** Three smooth stone require six smelts: **one furnace-minute**, not six. Two blast furnaces require **12 smelts**, not the build-time table’s six. Replacing two of six normal furnaces yields **2,880 ore/hour**, versus 2,160—not twice the workshop throughput. The average copper backlog takes approximately **13.7 ticking minutes**, versus 18.2. **Fix:** distinguish individual-furnace speed, total furnace work, and workshop elapsed time throughout the tables.

11. **P2 — §5: Load/collect scheduling and reconciliation need an explicit state model.** “Empty or same item” does not establish input capacity, fuel capacity, output compatibility, remaining fuel coverage, or recipe eligibility. Fuel `ceil(n/8)` assumes coal/charcoal. Meanwhile `load − collect − contents = 0` cannot hold indiscriminately across raw-to-ingot conversion and fuel consumption. **Fix:** define per-furnace recipe assignment, capacity-aware loads, recipe-specific furnace eligibility, production reservations, and separate material/fuel balances. Include full output, residual fuel, interrupted transfer, and unloaded-furnace recovery cases.

12. **P2 — §§1.3, 6–7: The exposure and time-budget claims overstate available capacity.** Being near home does not mean a bot is available for project work. A 30% decision budget also does not bound body time when one decision occupies a furnace for roughly 80 seconds. Distance to home does not establish ticking exposure for furnaces sited elsewhere; Paper distinguishes loaded chunks from ticking chunks. **Fix:** measure actual project body-seconds and furnace-location exposure, with elapsed load-to-collect observations. Treat the current time estimate as an upper bound on nearby bot-time. [Paper chunk diagnostics](https://docs.papermc.io/paper/reference/commands/)

13. **P2 — §7: Liveness and positive controls are too weak for promotion.** One order or placement can pass liveness while the chain never delivers a usable result. Stage 5 can emit demand without exercising an actual threatened prune. The weekly “previous week and fleet” comparison also reintroduces uncontrolled comparisons if used to judge a change. **Fix:** require stage-specific end-to-end exposure, such as smelt-and-bank, completed structure, load-transform-collect, and a demonstrated protected disposal decision. Add failing fixtures for every defect gate, explicit INCONCLUSIVE deadlines, and pre/post randomized-control DiD for causal conclusions. Keep completion demonstrations and weekly descriptive trends labelled separately.

14. **P2 — §§8–9: Floor/project reservations need atomic arbitration.** Independently checking that the other plan has no occupied cells permits both planners to observe an empty site and publish overlapping plans. An unfinished floor plan can also exclude a site indefinitely. **Fix:** use a common town siting lease or atomic reservation publication, specify precedence and expiry/abandonment semantics, and preserve reservations for already-built cells. Test simultaneous siting and restart between reservation and publication.

15. **P2 — §§7, 9: Staging and rollback are not yet independently reviewable.** The record’s fixed cursor advances into later blueprints, but stage-specific enablement is unspecified. Stage 2 already needs part banking; stage 3 introduces autonomous stock transformations; reverting producer code leaves physical structures and furnace contents behind. Those dependencies are missing from the effort estimate. **Fix:** define enabled blueprints/jobs per stage, persistent protection across rollback, compatible record readers, and recovery of outstanding furnace contents and claims. Re-estimate after incorporating elevated construction, shared accounting, and recovery work; the fleet-wide one-day completion claim is currently unsupported.

VERDICT: CHANGE

**Geometry assessment**

The central terracing sequence is sound under plinth’s stated rules:

- Ring feet at `s+2` give eyes at `s+3.62`, above the wall-top face at `s+3`.
- Interior roof cells can attach to roof side faces from the pad; no bottom-face click is needed.
- A step block at `s+2` on the ring provides feet elevations `s+2 → s+3 → s+4`.
- Parapet blocks at `s+4` can be placed from roof stands.

My flat-ground probe placed **28 roof cells from ring stands and 35 from pad stands**, with `standOk` walk-back checks. This was a geometry probe, not a complete blueprint or Paper validation.

**P3 — §§4.0, 4.3:** Freeze the parapet’s single gap opposite the stair, distinguish permitted transit through that entrance from placement stands near unguarded edges, and validate lantern clicks against actual wall shapes. These belong in the promised complete-sequence and Paper gates.

**Remaining and newly introduced findings**

1. **P2 — §§3.2–3.3, 5: Stock visibility and delivery still lack a complete prerequisite contract.**  
   The existing journal counts targeted groups, not the copper, fuel, and finished parts that admission and stage-1 eligibility require. Furnace-slot counting in stage 3 does not supply that earlier chest census. Also, `barrel` being `PROTECTED` does not make it bankable: it is absent from both the existing standing bank set and the proposed `PROJECT_PARTS`.

   **Fix:** Assign complete project-relevant chest counting to stage 1, extend it as materials become enabled, and include barrels in project banking from stage 4. Test interrupted production followed by deposit and another bot’s withdrawal.

2. **P2 — §§3.2–3.3: A town disposal floor is not a carried-material allocation hold.**  
   Raising `target'` protects chest stock against pruning, but does not reserve a helper’s bag against ordinary crafting or scaffolding. Scaffold exclusion applies only during builder visits. Admission also counts the bot’s bag without excluding its escape/material reserves. Thus “the whole BOM allocates” can include stock the project cannot safely spend.

   **Fix:** Allocate only spendable stock after reserves, and carry allocation ownership through withdrawal, transformation, interruption, and deposit. Apply material protection to every job carrying an allocation, with explicit release/reconciliation rules.

3. **P2 — §§3.5, 5: Room-making still has a deterministic dead end.**  
   “Town deposit runs first” does not imply that it frees space: a full bag can contain reserves or otherwise unbankable items. `roomOrder` currently depends on the milestone’s `ready.noRoom`; a project refusal does not activate it. `smelt_town` can therefore remain ineligible forever, and it has no project abandonment state to close that loop.

   **Fix:** Feed project room requirements into an executable room-making order, and define bounded retry/alternative selection for inaccessible stations. Test the full chain when deposit legitimately frees zero slots.

4. **P2 — §§5, 7: The new furnace balance equation rejects normal operation.**  
   `inputs loaded = outputs collected + input still in` fails as soon as one item cooks but remains in the output slot. Separately, the stage-1 per-order equation omits opening furnace contents and recovered items retained in the bag after interrupted deposit. Existing `smelt` explicitly collects pre-existing output and drains slots; a lease serializes that behavior but does not attribute those items to the new order.

   **Fix:** Reconcile opening inventory plus confirmed transfers against closing input, closing output, collections, and returned inventory, normalized by recipe. Track recovered prior stock separately from new production. Define outstanding production reservations so multiple furnace assignments cannot independently satisfy the same project shortfall.

5. **P2 — §§3.4, 8: Interim siting remains non-atomic.**  
   A project can read an empty floor/plinth record while that planner reads an empty project record, then both publish overlapping sites. Three-cell clearance does not prevent this interleaving. An owner-defined expiry also provides no expiry when the older planner has no abandonment rule.

   **Fix:** Make common arbitration a prerequisite for concurrent siting, or disable competing siters during project site publication. Specify retirement of stale unbuilt reservations.

6. **P2 — §§3.1, 8–9: Rollback recovery is asserted rather than ensured.**  
   Accepting possible structure destruction is an explicit policy choice. However, “ordinary `smelt` drains any furnace it opens” does not guarantee recovery: reverting stage 3 to stage 2 retains ordinary-smelt yard exclusion, and `smelt_town` need not become eligible when the remaining copper is already cooked inside those furnaces.

   **Fix:** Retain a drain-only recovery path in the rollback baseline, including reconciliation and release of outstanding claims. Document any deliberate abandonment separately from guaranteed recovery.

7. **P3 — §§4.1, 5: Two numerical statements remain wrong.**  
   Each blast furnace requires **three cobblestone and six smelts** for its three smooth stone; the pair requires six cobblestone and twelve smelts. The stage-1 **2.3–2.8 bot-hours/world** divides the fleet estimate by 16 while labelling it as the twelve shared worlds. Their actual copper subtotal is not supplied.

   **Fix:** Correct the blast-furnace input and report shared-world processing estimates from the shared-world census. The main placement totals—70, 229, 286—and hall-M total of 687 smelts are otherwise internally consistent.

8. **P3 — §§3.4, 5, 8: Specify lease ownership and dig-exception authority at integration.**  
   `smelt_town` says it acquires a furnace lease, while every `smelt` caller must acquire one too. Define one acquisition owner or explicit token passing. Likewise, the dig guard should authorize the actual survival operation or owning builder’s planned clear, not merely trust a broad telemetry purpose label.

   **Fix:** Cover nested smelting, interruption/recovery, and misattributed dig contexts in the stage reviews. The overall lease and central-guard designs are sound.

**New findings introduced by r3**

1. **P2 — §§3.4, 4.0: The new spatial-disjointness boundary excludes the plinth shell, but not its stairs.**

   `wellSpiral` searches to Chebyshev radius 14. On the specified branch, `stairOptions` places stair columns at offsets `2 + j`, with `j` up to 4. A cap at radius 14 can therefore have stairs reaching radius 20. Project cells beginning at radius 17 can overlap those stairs. Reading `plan` and `prev[]` covers published plans, not a simultaneously planned stair.

   **Fix:** Derive exclusion domains from the entire possible footprint, including stairs and required clearance, or require shared arbitration before concurrent siting. Make the floor planner’s footprint agreement a prerequisite, rather than a requested confirmation.

2. **P2 — §§5, 7: The replacement furnace equations reject legitimate input recovery.**

   `O.input + loaded = C.input + cooked` has no term for input taken back. For example, opening with eight foreign input items and draining them produces `8 = 0 + cooked`; with no output produced, the output equation requires `cooked = 0`. The same contradiction occurs when an interrupted job retrieves its own uncooked input. Existing `smelt` explicitly takes foreign input; `yard_drain` also requires this operation.

   **Fix:** Record confirmed input removals and use:

   `O.input + loaded = C.input + cooked + input_taken`

   Keep the output equation, normalize by recipe, and include cursor/recovery transfers in the inventory boundary. Test foreign-input draining and cancellation before the first item cooks.

3. **P2 — §§5, 7: The revised loss-only gate can conceal losses it already has enough information to detect.**

   Closing furnace contents are already included in “units out,” yet a shortfall is tolerated up to “in-flight items.” That grants the same inventory a second allowance. Separately, counting only prior stock *collected* on the input side while counting all closing furnace stock on the output side creates apparent gains when prior stock remains. A one-sided gate ignores those gains, allowing them to offset actual losses.

   **Fix:** Use one consistent boundary: all opening bag/furnace inventory plus confirmed withdrawals, against deposits and all closing inventory. Once closing input and output are counted, cooking needs no additional material allowance. Treat uncertain reads as unreconciled exposure, not successful conservation. Test loss masked by retained opening stock and by uncooked closing input.

4. **P3 — §§3.4, 5: Time-based reservation expiry needs stock reconciliation before replacement production.**

   Expected completion plus 30 minutes can expire while a furnace still holds the reserved input or output. The design separately acknowledges uncertain ticking exposure. Expiry alone must not make that same shortfall eligible for another load.

   **Fix:** At expiry, inspect and reconcile the furnace, retain attribution for surviving stock, and release only the uncovered remainder. Test delayed collection and an unloaded furnace during stage 4.

5. **P3 — §§3.5, 5, 7: Generalised room-making needs project attribution and budget coverage.**

   The room order runs before project jobs. Unless its request retains the project’s identity, repeated wear-out, compost, deposit, or well work can escape the ten-minute project body-time budget and its exposure accounting.

   **Fix:** Carry the requesting job and measured slot requirement through room-order issue and outcome; charge project-triggered preparation to the project budget while preserving milestone priority. Validate the zero-slots-freed chain in stage 1.

6. **P3 — §§8–9: A reflex-held body is an integration authority, not automatically proof that the particular dig belongs to that reflex.**

   The code distinguishes runner skill claims from reflex arbiter grants, including a legacy path. Checking a current holder alone could exempt a stale discretionary operation still unwinding after preemption.

   **Fix:** Specify the actual grant/operation authority used by the wrapper and validate stale-skill digs, reflex `goto` digs, legacy behavior, and owning-builder clears. This is an integration gate, not a reason to reject the standalone guard architecture.

The seven-stage split is otherwise reasonable. The complete-sequence proof and Paper gates remain the appropriate place to settle parapet access, lantern clicks, barrel placement, and terrain-specific construction behavior.

VERDICT: CHANGE

**New findings introduced by r4 only**

1. **P2 — §§3.4, 5, 8: The new baseline drain cannot deliver its promised rollback recovery.**

   The new predicate selects output **≥8**, abandoned input, or MIXED contents. A furnace containing **1–7 output items, no input, and no foreign contents** satisfies none of these conditions. After stage-4 rollback removes `furnace_collect`, that stock can remain indefinitely. Fuel-only leftovers likewise have no drain trigger.

   The spatial guarantee also fails: the outer domain permits Chebyshev distance 44, whereas existing `TOWN_RADIUS` checks use horizontal Euclidean distance. A furnace at offset `(40,40)` is legal in the outer domain but approximately **56.6 blocks** from home, outside the promised 48-block recovery scan.

   **Fix:** Give orphaned/done furnaces an eventual drain trigger for any remaining contents, independent of batch size. Make the yard’s entire furnace footprint fit the recovery scan, or make the baseline scan cover the entire permitted domain. Verify small-output, fuel-only and diagonal-yard rollback cases.

2. **P2 — §§5, 7: The new between-session timing inequality rejects valid partial cooking.**

   `k <= elapsed / 10 s` assumes cooking starts at zero progress when the previous session closes. It need not: Mineflayer exposes partial cooking through [`furnace.progress`](https://github.com/PrismarineJS/mineflayer/blob/master/docs/api.md#furnaceprogress).

   For example, close with one input already nine seconds cooked; reopen two seconds later with that item completed. The balances agree and nothing was lost, but the new gate rejects `1 > 2/10`. Section 7 makes this a deterministic REVERT condition. Its table also omits §5’s five-second blast-furnace exception.

   **Fix:** Include opening cook progress in the timing bound, or use a conservative bound allowing one partially cooked item, with the furnace-specific duration and observation uncertainty. Keep the exact material conservation equations unchanged. Verify a session boundary immediately before completion.

These are errors in the specified predicates and equations, rather than unresolved Paper behavior. Construction sequencing and the remaining dig-authority integration check stay P3.

I checked the recipes against minecraft-data 1.21.8 and they all match: barrel, cut_copper 4→4, copper_bulb needs a blaze_rod, and none of the four copper_* items in §2 exist on 1.21.8. Most of the BOM and throughput arithmetic also holds. The problems are in how the design meets existing code.

1. **P1, §4 stands and §4.1–4.3.** No cell at s+3 or higher can get its first block under the planner the design adopts. In plinth's `standFor` (origin/plinth-on-8cd0bc6), a top-face click needs the bot's eye above the face (`if (topFace && eye.y <= point.y) continue`). From the pad, the eye is at s+2.62. The third wall course and the corner-log tops are placed on faces at s+3; the roofs are at s+4 (storehouse, workshop) and s+5 (hall). A cell's first block has no side neighbour to click instead, and §4 forbids standing on walls or the roof. So every project stalls with its roof open. **Fix:** before estimating stage 2, either design a raised-stand mechanism (an interior step or scaffold, removed afterwards, with its own fall analysis), or prove on Paper that a click on a top face below eye level is accepted and drop the sight rule for that case. Re-estimate the 6–9 days after.

2. **P1, §3.3 "junk list unchanged" and §4.3/§4.2 v1b.** `stone_bricks` and `smooth_stone` are already junk: they are in `SCAFFOLD_DECORATIONS`, so in `WELL_JUNK` (well.mjs:106), and the owner named stone_bricks as junk on 10-07. They are also pathfinder scaffold blocks (scaffold.mjs:106), as are planks and logs. The doc's own test ("no blueprint intersects the junk list") fails for hall@1 (88 bricks) and v1b (smooth stone). As written, the well throws away crafted parts and the pathfinder spends carried materials. **Fix:** add owner decision D8 (take them off the well list), or build the hall from cobble. Remove project materials from the builder's scaffold set during a visit.

3. **P1, §5 stage-1 SMELT job.** The design is three orders (withdraw, smelt, town_deposit) spread across decisions. Town deposit runs first at 34+ slots, and the median bag is at 34. `raw_copper` and `copper_ingot` are in `DEPOSIT_ALWAYS` (bankable.mjs:424), so withdrawn copper goes straight back into the chest before `smelt` can run. Separately, coal and charcoal are in `IRON_LADDER`, which the town deposit never banks, so the town's charcoal fuel reserve can never fill and the charcoal job fires indefinitely. **Fix:** make it one composite skill (withdraw, smelt, deposit inside one budget, like `withdraw_pick`), or add a job-hold keep that town deposit honours. Drop charcoal from stage 1.

4. **P2, §3.3 stage 5.** The new groups at "target 0 plus demand" break in two ways:
   - Target 0 counts as at-target by definition, so `stockWellStacks` sends copper_block and cut_copper above the 64 reserve down the well whenever demand drops to 0 (stalled, complete, or not yet sited).
   - `barrel` matches `PROTECTED`, and stocktarget.test asserts that no group names a protected item.

   **Fix:** leave project parts untargeted and add them to `PROTECTED` and `NEVER_DISPOSE`.

5. **P2, §3.1 and D2.** Everything the project has to work with is keyed by pool: the claims and count journal (`townDir = poolStateDir(memory.pool)`, skills.mjs:3140), the well site, the composter site and plinth's record. A world-keyed project in an isolated world faces five separate claim journals over the same chests, so draw claims do not exclude each other. **Fix:** recommend "none in v1" for D2 (simplest), or re-key town state by world first.

6. **P2, §8 `smelt` row and §7 gates.** `smelt` takes the nearest furnace within 32 blocks (skills.mjs:9473). It takes that furnace's output, pulls out any input that isn't its own (9673–9676), and empties the furnace on exit. In stage 1, two bots sharing a town furnace makes the "items lost across an order" gate REVERT on what is only a transfer. In stage 3, an iron rung (which always wins) will empty workshop furnaces. Likewise, "an item lost in a visit" fires when the pathfinder spends carried blocks as scaffold. **Fix:** use the furnace lease from stage 1, exclude workshop furnaces from `smelt`, and reconcile the loss gates per furnace or town and against place events, not per order.

7. **P2, overclaims and arithmetic.**
   - §3.5 "cannot happen for a sited project" is too strong: prune, model deposits and withdraws, and the bag counted as town stock can all take stock away. Say "rare, and it stalls".
   - The average world holds about 656 raw copper, but hall M needs 570/0.8 = 712 under the 80% rule. The average world gets S, which contradicts §5's "enough for one hall M".
   - Hall raw copper is 579, not 570: 16 crafts × 4 blocks = 576, plus 3 for the rod.
   - v1b gives 1.33x, not "halves" (4×360 + 2×720 = 2,880 vs 2,160 per hour).
   - Six smooth stone is 12 smelts, about 2 furnace-minutes, not 6.
   - "128 furnaces/day left where smelt put them" counts crafts, not placements (68 of 80 bots carry one, and deposits of furnaces are seen). Measure place rows before making this stage 3's primary.

8. **P2, §7 liveness and denominators.**
   - The siting rate is unknown, and wellsite found no site in 5 of 10 towns. Run the site predicate on all 16 worlds' region files (wellsite-replay) before stage 2, report n/16, and canary only a pool whose town has a site.
   - Report what share of at-town decisions currently reach the slot after the room order with `!order`. That share, not the 58 bot-hours, is the real time available.
   - A stalled storehouse never advances the cursor, and there is no rule for moving to `abandoned`. Stages 3 and 4 can then show nothing. Define stalled → abandoned → re-site or skip.

9. **P3, simplify.**
   - Drop COMPACT from stage 1: storage is only 23% used, and `copper_block` is not in `DEPOSIT_ALWAYS`, so compacting moves copper out of town and into bags.
   - Consider making stage 2 the workshop's furnace line on a pad with no roof. Nothing is above s+2, it delivers the owner's copper request first, and it reverses D7.

10. **P3, smaller items.**
    - The workshop's `in` and `fuel` barrels will receive general town deposits, because `CONTAINER_RE` includes barrel. Drop them.
    - The sapling keep-out covers only its column. A grown canopy pushes logs into the footprint, which reads as foreign and stalls the project.
    - Lightning strikes on the rod remove oxidation from nearby copper. This is cosmetic, and the §3.2 families already handle it.

R1. **P2, §3.4 and §4.0: the interim siting rule puts the storehouse outside "town storage".**
- Every definition of town storage is a 16-block cylinder around home:
  - `inTown` uses `STORAGE_NEAR = 16` (chestfull.mjs:64, composter.mjs:207);
  - the count scan keeps only containers inside it (skills.mjs about line 3181);
  - the town deposit fires only within 16 of home and reaches containers within 16 of the bot.
- Projects must sit beyond Chebyshev 16, so the storehouse's barrels would be uncounted (blind to 3.3a and to allocation) and unreachable by the town deposit.
- Stage 5's "≥1 deposit into its barrels" could then only come from the model's own `deposit`.
- **Fix:** either count a project record's container cells as town storage explicitly, and let the town deposit target them; or keep the storehouse until plinth and floor adopt the siting file, so it can sit inside 16. The yard and hall are fine where they are.

R2. **P2, §3.5 and stage 1: the generalised `roomOrder` is mis-described.**
- `roomOrder` only issues `eat`, `deposit` and `home` (workorder.mjs `ROOM_ORDER_OF`). wear_out, compost and the well are not room-order kinds.
- A deposit remedy driven by a project must not dig into the town deposit's keeps, or it fights the milestone ladder.
- **Fix:** drop it from stage 1. Below 2 free slots a project job simply skips, the stall rule bounds the wait, and the dry run measures how often it happens.

R3. **P2, §5 furnace accounting: the equations will trip the stage-4 REVERT gate on routine sessions.**
- The equations have no term for input or fuel taken out during a session. `smelt` and `yard_drain` both take leftover input and fuel back out, which breaks them.
- Load-and-leave cooking happens between visits, not inside one.
- **Fix:**
  - write the per-session balance as `O.input + loaded = C.input + cooked + inputTaken`, with the same for fuel;
  - add a continuity check from each close to the next open: cooked between visits must be ≥ 0 and no more than the elapsed time ÷ 10 s.

R4. **P3, stage 2 exposure.** townzone-01 already refuses discretionary digs inside the square, and that is where the plinth and well are. So "≥5 refused discretionary digs" may never happen, and the canary closes INCONCLUSIVE by construction. Count only digs townzone passed, and take the base rate from `_dig_town` before launch.

R5. **P3, stage-5 holds and bag room.** In towns with a usable well, a hold that keeps disposal floor above the town's cobble count stops the well from throwing any bag cobble while the project runs. Bags stay full, which can starve the ≥2-free-slot jobs. Say so, and report it in the read.

R6. **P3, interim siting area.** The 17–44 band is where trees grow and where planting happens, so the 3-cell tree keep-out may make sites scarce. The n/12 census will measure this. The site rules otherwise do not conflict with the plinth, well or floor (all within about 14 of home) or with time spent at town (36.9% of bot-time is 16–48).

R7. **P3, stage 1 scope.** Stage 1 changes the milestone `smelt` (the lease, `furnace_busy`) for every bot, not only the new job. The rung-completion DiD covers it. Name the iron rung's `smelt` successes as the specific control row.

The 7-stage plan, its dependencies and the 9–13 week calendar look honest.
