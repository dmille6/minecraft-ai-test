# Night of 2026-08-07 → 08

Nine defects, three of them found only after two or three attempts. Every one
was found by measuring after a deploy, not by reasoning before one — which is
the finding, not an aside.

## The nine

### 1. `unstick` reported success 74 times out of 74

Its success test was `escapedCell()` — "am I on a different block than I started
on". Inside a pit that is satisfied perfectly by stepping from one corner to the
other. The same escape square was chosen **8 times** (`5,74,-3`), 6 times
(`3,73,-5`), 5 times (`-14,68,-4`). The bot was walking a circuit of a hole it
could not climb out of; in-world it reads as hopping on the spot.

Fixed: per-bot memory of tried squares, and three unsticks within four blocks is
no longer three successes — it is the horizontal escape being the wrong escape,
escalating to `pillarOut`.

**Test:** `bots/test/unstick.test.mjs` builds the actual 2×2 pit geometry.

### 2. `pillarOut` placed one block per invocation

It ended on `if (!isEntombed(bot)) break` — and `isEntombed()` means *head
blocked*. Miner01 sat at the bottom of a 40-block shaft it had dug itself, head
open to the sky, **for ninety minutes**, with 70 cobblestone and two pickaxes in
its inventory. Every invocation placed exactly one block and returned.

Fixed: the loop ends when `canStartAPath()` succeeds. Height gained is progress,
not success.

### 3. The lessons store gate-locked the entire tech tree

100% of `craft` failures were `missing_ingredients` — the right action attempted
at a moment the bot had no inputs. Thirteen of them were `cannot craft
oak_planks -- needs 1x oak_log (you have 4x oak_planks)`: failing to make a
thing it already had.

```
                Gather01  Miner01  Gather02  Scout01  Scout02
craft wooden_pickaxe   54       34        20        4       17
craft oak_planks       13       47         –       13        7
craft stick             –        –        37       23       19
gather oak_log          –        4         1        3        –
```

Every rung of `log → planks → stick → pickaxe` blocked, and the gate blocked the
only actions that could produce the success which would clear the counter.
30 watchdog stagnation events in 15 minutes; one bot moved 0.0 blocks in 166s.

Fixed across seven changes — gap-gating, wall-clock decay, an allowlist instead
of a denylist, a widened disproof channel, and a gate that may not block a
producer of what its own milestone needs. **Test:** `bots/test/livelock.test.mjs`,
18 cases including the exact Miner01 sequence.

### 4–6. Drowning, three attempts

Scout01 drowned 17 times while `reflex: drowning` fired **zero** times.

1. **The counter was trusted over the world.** Added a guard requiring the head
   to be in water or in a block. Correct for the case it was written for.
2. **Water counted as a wall.** `isEntombed()` tested `name !== 'air'`, so
   underwater every bot is permanently entombed — and the escape held the serial
   reflex loop for 20–30s placing blocks into the sea while the oxygen check got
   no tick. Fixed; entombed events went to zero. **Bots kept drowning.**
3. **The rescue was behind a log throttle.** `throttled('oxygen')` was evaluated
   in one condition and again in its `else if`. The first call consumed the
   token, that condition rejected on `!reallyLosingAir`, and the second returned
   false. The swim-up was inside that unreachable branch.

After the third: 0 deaths, 5 reflexes in 3 minutes.

**Then a fourth layer.** `mineflayer-pathfinder` rewrites `jump` on every
`physicsTick` while a goal is set, so the swim was being overwritten within
50ms. Eight more drowning deaths in 45 minutes *after* the rescue worked.
`seizeBody()` now clears the goal and stops any dig before steering.

**Test:** `bots/test/drowning.test.mjs`, 14 cases. The throttle bug is one line:
ten consecutive ticks must all say `swim`.

### 7. 134 fall deaths were one hole

```
134 fall deaths — 133 of them (99%) in ONE 32×32 cell at x=0, z=0
all land at y=30-39 · fell_blocks median 45 · affects 5 different bots
exact landing spot: x=28, y=31, z=0 — 100 deaths on that block
```

A 1-block-wide vertical shaft, open from y=80 to a floor at y=30, solid on all
four sides — a bot-dug mineshaft 28 blocks from home that nobody covered. Not a
movement problem. One `/fill` of 46 blocks ended it; instance #1 has had zero
deaths since.

### 8. The model was never given a `y`

`worldfacts.mjs` rendered resource sightings as `${r.x},${r.z}` — **two
numbers** — while `goto` takes three. The model filled slots left to right, so
the z it was handed became the y it emitted. All 34 impossible-elevation
rejections were ours, not the model's. Hazards on the very next loop already
printed three numbers, so the prompt showed two coordinate conventions in one
list.

### 9. The A/B label did not label the experiment

`prompt.system_hash` hashed the **milestone**, not the system prompt — so every
arm of any prompt experiment would carry the same handful of values, keyed on
whichever objective happened to be active. Fixed to hash the prompt; measuring
it live showed **5 distinct hashes across 5 bots**, because the prompt opens
with the bot's name. Fixed again to normalise name and role out.

Neither failure was detectable after a run. Both produce plausible hashes, and
the experiment they labelled is simply lost. **Test:** `bots/test/abhash.test.mjs`.

## Two performance findings, both measured

**Prefix caching was impossible by construction.** The system prompt opened with
`You are ${config.bot.name}`, putting a unique token at position zero for every
bot. llama.cpp matches prefixes from token 0, so no two bots shared a single
cached token. Roughly 49% of every prompt is byte-identical across the fleet.

```
940-token prompt, two different bot names, measured on the Studio:
  identity FIRST:   BotAlpha 2.05s   BotBravo 2.17s    ← no sharing
  identity LAST:    BotAlpha 2.18s   BotBravo 0.19s    ← 11x on the second
```

**Inference was oversubscribed and it only showed in the tail.** Minimum cadence
is **N bots × GPU-seconds per decision**. Instance #1 ran 8 bots at 45s against
~8s/decision — a 1.4× overload that looked survivable at p50 8.2s. The tell was
`p99 == 180.0s`: the request timeout, ten times an hour, each a decision thrown
away. Raising to 70s took p90 from 30.1s to 6.8s with zero errors.

## What changed in how we work

**Stop discovering code properties on a live fleet.** Five of the nine were
provable in milliseconds. Each cost a deploy, a restart, and a measurement
window — and the restarts perturbed the trial. `scripts/preflight.sh` now gates
every deploy; `scripts/fixture-from-telemetry.py` turns a production failure
into a fixture and a test skeleton.

**Deploy once, then leave it alone.** Eleven code versions shipped in one
evening. The best sample was n=57 showing 60% success; it decayed to 37% at
n=194. Both numbers were honest. Only the second was useful.

**Two reviewers, working independently, catch different things.** ChatGPT found
that "125 bots of headroom" was wrong by two orders of magnitude. Claude found
that the impossible-elevation bug was our rendering, not the model ignoring
instructions. Neither would have surfaced from one pass, and each was wrong
about something the other caught — a claimed runner-pause bug that fires zero
times, and a proxy-vs-condition framing that missed the throttle.
