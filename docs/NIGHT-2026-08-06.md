## The pattern worth keeping

**Capability without its inverse**, six or more times: something can be added but
never removed, a guard trips with no path forward, a counter increments where
nothing clears it. Blocks that could never be disproved. A watchdog that could
start bots but never checked whether it should. A reflex that could fire but not
stand down.

**Recovery mechanisms that outlived their world**, three times: the watchdog
restarting units on a host the bots had left; the homepage linking to a viewer
that had moved; a rescue point chosen for terrain since dug away. Each was
correct when written and became harmful when the system changed, and none had a
check that it still applied.

**A watchdog whose threshold sits inside the p90 of legitimate work will spend
most of its firings on false positives.** Fourth timeout-nesting bug here.

## Measurement, and why it was wrong

Excluding reflex noise: 409 real skill attempts, 107 "successes" — 26%. But
**144 of 313 successes (46%) moved zero blocks and changed no inventory**. Real
rate: 13.9%. Every success figure quoted before 2026-08-06 is inflated ~2×.

That is ADR-0003, and it was then reproduced as a controlled experiment. Swapping
to a faster 7B model:

```
                  14B/Studio   coder:7b/5080
admit-rate            71%           78%
median latency      3083ms        1793ms
productive skills   10 of 16      6 of 31
```

**Both headline metrics improved while the agent got materially worse.**

### The measurement error I made

My inventory readout counted **slots, not quantities**. For hours I reported the
bots could not accumulate resources; Scout02 was holding 59 oak_log while I was
saying it had one. The gathering was never the problem — crafting was.

### A pattern worth adding

Twice tonight the **comment was right and the code had diverged from it**. The
`explore` heading said "away from spawn" and computed away from home; identical
until the colony moved, then silently wrong. Nothing checks that a comment and
its code still agree.

### Gap still open

A bot can be *functionally* stuck while *technically* moving. Gather01 spent five
checks blocked on every `gather` it knew, with `goto` stalling in all four
compass directions — but it shuffled a few blocks each attempt, so the watchdog's
"no movement for 12 minutes" test never tripped. Stuckness should be measured by
progress, not displacement.

### Fixing a skill does not clear its reputation

`explore` was unbounded-pathfinding broken and returned nothing but failures for
its first hour. By the time it was fixed, `learned_avoid` had recorded enough
failures that the model stopped choosing it entirely — twenty minutes of
telemetry with `explore` never selected once, while the fleet sat on a plateau
that `explore` existed to break.

The skill worked. Its reputation was from when it didn't.

This is the `status` problem inverted. There, no-ops were recorded as wins and
kept being chosen after the reward stopped. Here, a broken skill was recorded as
useless and kept being avoided after the bug was fixed. **Both are the learning
layer faithfully remembering a world that no longer exists.**

Cleared the stale `explore` entries (backups at `*.pre-explore-clear`) and it was
chosen again within five minutes.

Implication worth carrying forward: any deploy that changes a skill's behaviour
should invalidate that skill's accumulated judgements, the way a schema change
invalidates a cache. Nothing does this automatically today.
