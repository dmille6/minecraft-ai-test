# Tidy, unattended bot towns remain unclaimed

Yes, the platform is worth building, and the reason is narrow and specific. No open-source project, research paper or commercial product found combines **unattended multi-day operation, no cheats, tidy worlds and terraforming toward a plan, on one consumer GPU with a novice install**. The landscape divides into interactive frameworks (Mindcraft, minecraft-agent-swarm), research systems that chase completion benchmarks (Voyager, Odyssey, the October 2026 Gemini team that beat the game), pixel/VLA policies (JARVIS-VLA, Optimus-3, ROCKET-2), chat-only NPC plugins, and companion products (Player2). Commercial money has left autonomous Minecraft societies: Altera, which ran Project Sid, now sells office agents. Building beauty and terrain have only a few benchmarks and no system aimed at them, and no paper found studies agents that clean up after themselves. The main risk is not competition. It is that the platform's value rests on hard, unglamorous engineering in pathing, building and recovery, on a mineflayer plugin layer that is thinly maintained, while small local models still trail frontier models on embodied tasks. The 15.6M-decision log with judged answer keys is the platform's most defensible asset, because nobody else has comparable data on model choice.

## Everyone else optimises for a session, a benchmark or a chat

Mindcraft is the reference point: MIT, mineflayer, about **6.0k stars**, first-class Ollama support with its own fine-tuned Andy-4 models, and a skill library in which the LLM mostly chooses commands. Code generation is off by default ([GitHub](https://github.com/mindcraft-bots/mindcraft)). Its README says the project is "currently not very responsive to github issues". It warns against public servers with coding enabled ([README](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/mindcraft-bots/mindcraft/develop/README.md)), and the authors themselves call many viral clips "heavily dramatized" ([UCI](https://sites.uci.edu/kolbynottingham/2024/10/30/mindcraft)). It is built for interactive and task sessions, and no evidence was found of multi-day unattended runs. A community edition, mindcraft-ce, is active (157 stars, pushed 2026-11-19) ([GitHub](https://github.com/mindcraft-ce/mindcraft-ce)). Its local story is weak in practice: Lemonade found a 7B model attempted construction "with limited accuracy", while Mindcraft sends **2,100+ input tokens** for simple commands ([Lemonade](https://lemonade-server.ai/docs/server/apps/mindcraft)).

The closest peer in spirit is **minecraft-agent-swarm**: MIT, TypeScript, five role bots on one local `gpt-oss:20b`, a shared bulletin board, an "earn everything" rule, and a skill reliability tracker that retires broken skills ([GitHub](https://github.com/JesseRWeigel/minecraft-agent-swarm)). It is the only project that documents round-the-clock operation, and its numbers show where the hard part is. It needs **keepInventory with 10-55 deaths per day**, its food supply cannot sustain five bots, it relies on 240 s and 150 s watchdogs, and a stale lock file needs manual cleanup after a crash. It has 37 stars. That confirms the space is open, and it suggests that demand for unattended operation is not yet proven.

Research has moved further but in a different direction. Voyager's code-as-skill library, curriculum and self-verification remain the template ([GitHub](https://github.com/MineDojo/Voyager)), but the repo is tied to GPT-4 and Minecraft 1.19 and has not been pushed since 2024-04. Odyssey carries the idea to open models, with a LLaMA-3 fine-tuned on 390k+ wiki Q&A entries and 223 skills ([GitHub](https://github.com/zju-vipa/odyssey)). The headline 2026 result is that **four Gemini 4 Argon agents beat Minecraft in 27 h 14 min** using plain mineflayer. Along the way they wrote **2,783 programs and died 605 times** ([arXiv 2610.22319](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.22319)). That validates the mineflayer-plus-model architecture, and it shows the opposite value system: deaths and mess are free when the goal is completion. Project Sid scaled to 1,000+ agents with emergent taxes and religion, but its authors admit weak spatial awareness and physical coordination ([arXiv 2411.00114](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.00114)), and the agent code was not released.

Commercial efforts confirm the gap. Altera rebranded as Fundamental Research Labs and raised $30M to build agents for other markets ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2025/08/01/fundamental-research-labs-nabs-30-million-from-prosus-to-build-ai-agents-for-multiple-verticals)). Player2NPC ships companion entities, but they need the Player2 desktop app running as an AI gateway ([Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/player2npc)). The Citizens/Paper plugins (LLM NPC Lite, NeuroNPC, Craft Agent and others) talk but do not build ([Modrinth](https://modrinth.com/mod/llmnpc-lite)). DeepMind's SIMA 2 doubles task completion to 65% across games, MineDojo included ([DeepMind](https://deepmind.google/blog/sima-2-an-agent-that-plays-reasons-and-learns-with-you-in-virtual-3d-worlds/)), but it is a limited research preview, not something a user can run. The closest consumer analogue to a long-running local loop is NVIDIA ACE's inZOI Smart Zoi, which reflects overnight on an RTX card ([NVIDIA](https://www.nvidia.com/en-au/geforce/news/nvidia-ace-naraka-bladepoint-inzoi-launch-this-month/)). It shows that local-first agents are commercially credible.

## Four gaps nobody fills, and the evidence for each

The first gap is **leave-no-trace and terraforming toward a ground plan**. The only cleanup behaviour found anywhere is Mindcraft placing a temporary crafting table or furnace and collecting it back ([skills.js](https://github.com/mindcraft-bots/mindcraft/blob/develop/src/agent/library/skills.js)). The research survey found no paper on agents that clean or maintain a world, or on terraforming. Building has benchmarks, APT ([arXiv 2411.17255](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.17255)) and MineAnyBuild, whose creativity dimension scores aesthetics ([arXiv 2505.20148](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.20148v3)), but they score single structures, not a settled landscape. GDMC is the community that cares about terrain adaptation and aesthetics ([arXiv 2108.02955](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2108.02955)). Its generators write blocks directly through an HTTP mod, though, so they are admin edits by construction.

The second gap is **low-upkeep unattended operation as an engineering discipline**. Execution leases, progress budgets and bounded recovery have no counterpart in the frameworks. agent-swarm's watchdogs and manual lock cleanup are the state of the art, and the Gemini run tolerated hundreds of deaths. Parallelized Planning-Acting adds interruptible execution ([arXiv 2503.03505](https://arxiv.org/html/2503.03505v2)), but as a research harness, not an operations model.

The third gap is **coordination with minimal chatter**, where the research now agrees with the design. MineCollab found performance falls **up to 15%** when agents must communicate detailed plans ([arXiv 2504.17950](https://arxiv.org/abs/2504.17950)). Gated Coordination (April 2026) separates private execution state from public coordination state, updates memory only on system-verified outcomes, and escalates only when cost justifies it. It beats always-communicate baselines on long construction blueprints ([arXiv 2604.18975](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.18975)). A work board with reservations is the engineering form of that result, and no runnable project ships one: agent-swarm's bulletin board is the nearest.

The fourth gap is **evidence-based local model selection**. There is no controlled size-scaling study of Minecraft agents and no per-agent-hour cost data. The only distillation dataset found has **1,355 examples** and is called a proof of concept by its author, with rare actions weak ([Hugging Face](https://huggingface.co/datasets/SebastianAldrin/agent-society-distill-v1)). MindForge reports that open-weight models "still falter on elementary tasks" even after fine-tuning ([arXiv 2411.12977](https://arxiv.org/html/2411.12977v5)). Against that, 15.6M logged decisions with judged answer keys is about four orders of magnitude more data, and it is the one asset a competitor cannot copy quickly. Timelapses are a smaller but real gap: mature renderers exist, but none of them makes timelapses and no agent project offers them.

## Borrow the plumbing, own the builder and the judge

Licences decide most of the reuse choices, and Apache-2.0 accepts MIT freely. **Mindcraft's skills.js (MIT) is the richest action code to borrow**, but strip its cheat-mode `/setblock` paths. agent-swarm's skill reliability tracker and VM sandbox (MIT) map directly onto verified skill contracts. Odyssey's 183 compositional skills are MIT mineflayer JS but need porting from 1.19-era APIs. minecraft-mcp-server (Apache-2.0) offers clean tool wrappers ([GitHub](https://github.com/yuniko-software/minecraft-mcp-server)). Do not copy from ChatClef/AltoClef, which are AGPL ([CurseForge](https://www.curseforge.com/minecraft/mc-mods/chatclef)), or baritone-ts, also AGPL ([GitHub](https://github.com/nuxdie/baritone-ts)).

For the substrate, mineflayer is healthy (4.39.0, pushed 2026-11-14) ([GitHub](https://github.com/PrismarineJS/mineflayer)), but **mineflayer-pathfinder's npm release has been frozen at 2.4.5 since 2023** while its repo is still being pushed ([npm](https://www.npmjs.com/package/mineflayer-pathfinder)). Vendor or fork it rather than depend on the release, and keep an arrival-checking wrapper such as `walkToGoal`. No mature JS Baritone port exists. mineflayer-builder and prismarine-schematic are a reference, not a foundation ([GitHub](https://github.com/PrismarineJS/mineflayer-builder)), so scaffolding, ordering and reachability logic stays in-house. GDMC entries under MIT and Apache, such as Niels-NTG/GDMC2024 ([GitHub](https://github.com/Niels-NTG/GDMC2024)), are worth mining for heightmap analysis, plot selection, roads and levelling. Port the algorithms to produce a ground plan, then let bots execute the dig/fill difference through survival play.

For timelapses, run renderers as subprocesses so GPL never touches the codebase. Use **mcmap** (GPL-3.0, active) for isometric PNGs per project or town ([GitHub](https://github.com/spoutn1k/mcmap)), **uNmINeD** for fast top-down frames (licence unverified) ([unmined.net](https://unmined.net/)), **Chunky headless** for hero shots ([docs](https://chunky-dev.github.io/docs/reference/user_interface/chunky_launcher/headless)), and **BlueMap** (MIT) for interactive maps with incremental re-render ([GitHub](https://github.com/BlueMap-Minecraft/BlueMap)). Overviewer is stale. For model glue, put schema enforcement on the server side, through llama-server `json_schema`/GBNF ([GitHub](https://github.com/ggml-org/llama.cpp)), and validate with zod in Node through openai-node (Apache-2.0) ([GitHub](https://github.com/openai/openai-node)). That keeps llama.cpp, Ollama and vLLM interchangeable. Avoid instructor-js, which has been stale since January 2025.

For evaluation, adopt the judge pattern the literature has validated: a VLM critique of rendered views, checked against human labels. MCU's automatic evaluator reports **91.5% agreement with humans** ([arXiv 2310.08367](https://arxiv.org/html/2310.08367v4)), and Creative Agents pioneered pairwise build judging ([arXiv 2312.02519](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.02519)). Timelapse frames then serve twice, as showcase and as input to a beauty judge. Plancraft (MIT) ([GitHub](https://github.com/gautierdag/plancraft)) and MineAnyBuild tasks make cheap offline checks for challenger models, alongside the decision log.

## The risks are execution depth, small models and audience

**Small local models are the binding constraint.** The only full-game win used a frontier model. BALROG shows LLMs far from solving long-horizon games such as NetHack, and worse with image input ([arXiv 2411.13543](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.13543v2)). Ollama-specific breakage also bites: agent-swarm warns that qwen3-family models return empty output through Ollama's `/v1` ([GitHub](https://github.com/JesseRWeigel/minecraft-agent-swarm)). The platform's design answers this correctly, with deterministic verified skills doing the work and the model choosing among them. Its success therefore depends on how much the skill layer carries, not on the model. Optimus-3's claim that a domain-trained model beats general frontier VLMs on narrow skills ([arXiv 2506.10357](https://arxiv.org/abs/2506.10357)) suggests that a model distilled from the decision log could close part of the gap. That needs class balancing for rare actions.

**Substrate risk is real but manageable.** Mindcraft's README warns that Node v24+ breaks native dependencies and fixes dependency bugs with patch-package ([GitHub](https://github.com/mindcraft-bots/mindcraft)). The mineflayer plugins see light maintenance, and the platform will end up owning forks. A novice install also has to bundle a server, bots and a model runtime, which nobody else ships, so there is no proven recipe to copy.

**Audience risk is the least certain.** Altera's pivot is reported (secondary coverage, not verified) to reflect users finding agents "too autonomous" ([FRL Substack](https://digitalhumanity.substack.com/p/introducing-fundamental-research)), and agent-swarm's 37 stars suggest the unattended niche is small so far. Primary community threads on griefing, mess and cost were not retrieved, so demand for tidiness is an inference from the owner's goals rather than measured sentiment. Timelapses are the hedge: they turn a background process into something people watch and share.

**Competitive risk is low but not zero.** Frontier labs (SIMA 2, Genie 3) and well-funded teams could reach long-horizon building, but they are aiming at generalist agents and world models, not tidy local towns. A Mindcraft or mindcraft-ce contributor could add cleanup skills. That would be welcome under open source rather than a threat, as long as the platform's distinct parts (the operations model, the ground plan and the model-selection evidence) are what it is known for.

## Conclusion

The field has shown that mineflayer plus a capable model can do almost anything once, which moves the frontier to doing useful things **indefinitely, cheaply and without damage**. That is an operations and aesthetics problem, not a capability problem, and it is where this platform is aimed. Its strongest claims to novelty are the ones hardest to fake in a demo: bounded recovery, a debt-tracked clean world, and a defensible answer to "which local model, and why". Publishing the decision dataset or its judged answer keys, even in part, would make the platform the reference point for local Minecraft agent evaluation, a role nobody holds.

The practical posture follows from that. Treat Mindcraft, agent-swarm and Odyssey as MIT skill quarries, GDMC as an algorithm source for the ground plan, and the render tools as external processes. Spend original effort on the builder, the leases and budgets, the work board, and the judge. One open question deserves measurement before more investment: whether players want a town that tends itself. The timelapse pipeline is the cheapest way to find out.
